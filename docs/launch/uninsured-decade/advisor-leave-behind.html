The Uninsured Decade

The one retirement risk that isn't on your balance sheet.

The Bill, In Six Numbers

  • 42% Lifetime risk of dementia after 55 (35% men / 48% women); for a couple, about 2 in 3 odds that one of you faces it. (Nature Medicine, 2025)
  • $405,262 Average lifetime cost of dementia care for one person. (Alzheimer's Association, 2026 Facts and Figures, 2024 dollars)
  • 70% Share of that cost paid by the family — out of pocket or unpaid caregiving hours. (Alzheimer's Association)
  • ~$525,000 A ten-year staged cost model, early in-home support through late-stage memory care. (CareScout/Genworth 2025 Cost of Care Survey)
  • <12 Carriers still actively selling new standalone long-term-care insurance, down from 100+ around 2000. (American Academy of Actuaries, 2025)
  • 63% Higher four-year mortality rate for a spouse under sustained caregiving strain. (Schulz & Beach, JAMA, 1999)

The $0 Line

Medicare pays $0 toward custodial or memory care. Its own rule: "Medicare doesn't cover custodial care if it's the only care you need." (medicare.gov/coverage/long-term-care; 42 CFR §411.400)

The LTC Market

Standalone LTC policies have seen repeated double-digit rate increases — some carriers have filed for 60–100%+ increases on existing policyholders — and underwriting has tightened sharply. By the time the need feels real, a new policy is often unaffordable or unavailable. (S&P Global Market Intelligence, 2026 rate-filing data)

What You Can Still Underwrite

The only coverage that pays before the claim: the same premium that lowers the odds of the bill pays a dividend now — sleep, strength, energy, a retirement decade spent on your feet.

  • Run your own spend-down number. Net worth ÷ your state's annual private-pay care cost = roughly how many years your assets alone would cover before Medicaid eligibility.
  • Put a plan in writing now, while everyone involved can still weigh in — powers of attorney, health-care directives, a family conversation before a crisis forces it.
  • Work your own modifiable risk factors — sleep, movement, and metabolic health are linked in the research to a meaningfully lower personal risk profile. Expected-value thinking, not a guarantee.
