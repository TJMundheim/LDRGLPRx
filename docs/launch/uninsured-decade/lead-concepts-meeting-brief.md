# Meeting brief — Chris Wear, Lead Concepts (point of the spear)

_Drafted 2026-09-08 for TJ's meeting with Chris Wear (owner, Lead Concepts; ~30 yrs seminar direct-mail, nationwide; ~100K mailouts/week across annuity / retirement-income / living-trust / estate-attorney clients). Prior relationship: TJ used Lead Concepts for a regenerative-medicine seminar (Dr. Molina / MD Specialty Group). Friend._

## What we want (in order of size)
1. **Introductions to his seminar clients** for the Channel 0 piggyback: 8-minute guest slot before the host's close + a book on every seat + one QR slide. Nothing sold from the stage; the host's close gets easier.
2. **A piece inside his mailouts.** The invitation already goes to 55+ HNW households. A one-line/one-panel add ("Guest speaker: The Uninsured Decade — the one retirement risk your plan doesn't cover") or a card insert with the QR. Even a fraction of 100K/week is more of the right people than any channel on our list.
3. **His read on the room.** Which client types respond to which framing (annuity vs. trust vs. Medicare-supp), which metros, what response-rate lift a guest speaker has produced historically.

## What's in it for Chris (lead with this)
- **His clients' seminars convert better.** A credible guest who raises the stakes of the retirement conversation ("Medicare pays $0 for custodial care; the LTC market collapsed; two-in-three odds one of you faces it") makes the host's one-on-one booking feel urgent. Higher appointment rates = happier clients = retained accounts for him.
- **Differentiation for his invitations.** Every mailbox has three steak-dinner invites. "Guest speaker + free book" is a reason to pick this one. He can offer it as a premium option to his clients.
- **We are a client too.** When we run our own mailouts (Channel 8, phase 2), they run through Lead Concepts.
- **Zero risk to his brand.** No product from the stage, no MLM, DC credential stated plainly, disclaimers on every piece, gender-neutral, no competing financial product.

## Questions to ask Chris
1. Which of his clients run the most frequent dinners in DFW / Texas? Who would say yes fastest?
2. Would he pilot 3 dinners with one client and measure appointment-rate lift vs. their baseline?
3. Can a guest-speaker line or insert go on the invitation, and what does that cost / who approves it (his client)?
4. What framing does his audience respond to — "protect the estate," "protect the spouse," or "the one risk your plan doesn't cover"?
5. Compliance posture: what do his annuity/trust clients need to see before letting a guest on stage (disclaimer, credential, no-sell pledge)?
6. What would make this worth his time: referral fee per booked dinner, revenue share on books, or simply his clients' results?

"Introduce me to three of your clients for a pilot: I take eight minutes before their close, leave a book on every seat, and we measure whether their appointment rate goes up."
