VIDEO REQUIREMENTS

Both videos, starting and final, follow the same rules.

- Full body in frame, head to feet. Feet off the ground for every rep.

- One continuous take, no cuts or edits. Phone propped up, not held.

- Start from a dead hang with arms fully extended. Pull up until your chin is clearly above the bar. Hold that position with your chin above the bar while bringing your feet above the bar, as demonstrated in my video, then return to full arm extension. A rep counts only when you complete the full movement. Reps that do not meet the standard do not count.

- The set ends at true max effort: your last rep is visibly slow or you drop off the bar. A set that ends fast and clean looks like holding back, and holding back on the starting video is grounds for disqualification.

- The starting video must show a visible date, like a phone clock or a newspaper, filmed in the same take.

- The final video must be filmed at the same bar from the same camera angle.

SCORING

Score = (final max reps - starting max reps) x bodyweight in pounds x age multiplier

- Only improvement counts, so a beginner and a veteran compete on the same footing.

- Both divisions use this exact formula. Women are ranked only against the other women in the women's division, not against me or the entrants in my division.

- Bodyweight rewards the work of moving more weight. Use your honest starting weight.

- Age multiplier: add 1% for every year over 30, with no cap. Age 40 is 1.10, age 60 is 1.30, age 70 is 1.40, and it keeps going. Age 30 and under is 1.00. The idea is inspired by masters track and field age grading, which adjusts for age all the way to 100 because the decline after 50 and 60 is real. This formula does not use the official masters tables.

- Height is not a factor. Bodyweight already captures most of it, and every added factor makes the contest harder to defend.

- Tie on adjusted score within a division: the entry whose final video was entered first wins.

Example: a 200 lb, 40 year old who goes from 3 to 10 reps scores 7 x 200 x 1.10 = 1,540. A 150 lb, 25 year old who goes from 5 to 12 scores 7 x 150 x 1.00 = 1,050. And a 190 lb, 65 year old who goes from 4 to 11 scores 7 x 190 x 1.35 = 1,796, which beats the 40 year old. That is the point of the age multiplier: an older entrant who puts in the work can win it.

Same math for me in my division. I am 59 and about 235 lb, so my multiplier is 1.29. As a math example only, going from 5 to 15 valid reps would score 10 x 235 x 1.29 = about 3,032. My official starting count must come from a video meeting the full rep standard above. This example is not a verified starting count. The women's division is scored separately.

I am competing in my division under the same formula, video requirements and age bonus. No separate math for the host. I am 59 and still overcoming injuries, so the age adjustment matters to me too. We are not playing customer tennis here. I hope a lot of you challenge me, but it has to be fair to me as well. If my adjusted score beats the top challenger in my division, I keep that division's patches. That does not affect the separate women's prize. Women compete only against the other women.
