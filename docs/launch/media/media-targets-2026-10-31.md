## 1. Five pitch angles a producer would book

Timing is the reason to move this week:
- **November is National Alzheimer's Disease Awareness Month and National Family Caregivers Month.**
- Medicare open enrollment runs to **Dec 7**.
- **Thanksgiving is Nov 26.** That is the one week a year adult children sit across the table from their parents.

Morning shows book 1–3 weeks out, so Thanksgiving pitches have to go out by about Nov 10.

### Angle 1: The Uninsured Decade (money and consumer news)
- **News hook:** Medicare open enrollment ends Dec 7, and November is Family Caregivers Month. Most families still assume Medicare covers a parent's long-term memory care.
- **Headline:** "Medicare Pays $0 for Mom's Memory Care. Here's the Bill Nobody Told You About."
- **Talking points:**
  1. **The odds are family-sized.** Lifetime dementia risk after 55 is 42% (35% for men, 48% for women). For a couple, that is roughly two-in-three odds that at least one of them is affected (the book's derived figure).
  2. **The coverage gap.** Medicare pays $0 for long-term custodial care, the help with daily living that most dementia care actually is, and the private long-term-care insurance market has largely pulled back. The family pays.
  3. **The Thanksgiving question: "Who holds the folder?"** Find out where the documents are, who makes the decisions, and which advisor or attorney you should call. Endorse the professional; never compete with one. Up to 45% of dementia is linked to 14 modifiable factors, so the one "premium" a household can still pay is lowering its risk in midlife.
- **Outlet fit:** consumer and money desks (FOX 4 On Your Side, DMN Watchdog), public radio call-in shows (KERA Think, TPR The Source, Texas Standard), retirement and caregiving podcasts, Newsweek "My Turn".

### Angle 2: Alzheimer's starts in midlife (morning shows)
- **News hook:** Alzheimer's Awareness Month, plus the Thanksgiving table.
- **Headline:** "Alzheimer's Shows Up When You're Older. It Starts in Midlife." Tighter TV version: "The Disease That Starts Decades Before It Shows."
  - The "Alzheimer's starts in your 40s" version is punchier. It is safe only if TJ is comfortable defending "your 40s" on air; "midlife" is the locked wording.
- **Talking points:**
  1. **The newest number.** 42% lifetime risk after 55, and 48% for women (Nature Medicine, 2025). This is not a rare-disease story.
  2. **Up to 45% of dementia is linked to 14 modifiable factors** (Lancet 2024). That is a population figure, so it is a reason to act, not a guarantee.
  3. **Three things to start this week:**
     - shrink the belly fat (visceral fat drives inflammation, which raises cognitive risk);
     - protect sleep;
     - take a daily morning walk.

     Thanksgiving tag: what to notice gently in a parent, and in yourself.
- **Outlet fit:** FOX 4 Good Day, WFAA Good Morning Texas (editorial, not a sponsored slot), CBS Texas mornings, KERA's NTX Now, Texas Standard, midlife and menopause podcasts.

### Angle 3: Your AirPods can count your chews (tech and lifestyle)
- **Status: HOLD until the Chew Lab device test passes** (scheduled next session; docs/launch/cys/airpods-test-guide-2026-11-01.md).
  - Today the app runs on the simulator only.

- **News hook:** New Year's resolutions (pitch in early December for January), plus the GLP-1 era. It is a free, low-tech eating habit that rides on hardware people already own.
- **Headline:** "Your AirPods Can Count Your Chews, and It Might Shrink Your Plate."
- **Talking points:**
  1. **The tech.** AirPods carry motion sensors. The prototype reads the small head movements of chewing and counts each one. It is a live on-set demo with a plate of food.
  2. **The method.** Count easy things, not calories: 32 chews before the fork goes down. *Count Yourself Skinny* is built on simple counts anyone can keep.
  3. **The bigger picture.** Slower eating is a habit the GLP-1 generation will need when the prescription ends.
- **Outlet fit:** NBC 5 Texas Today, CultureMap, Fort Worth Inc. (local founder-builds-app), keto and weight-loss podcasts, later a tech segment on the DFW morning shows.

### Angle 4: GLP-1s and your brain (health desks)
- **News hook:** Semaglutide's two Phase 3 Alzheimer's trials (EVOKE and EVOKE+, 3,808 people with early Alzheimer's) missed their primary endpoint in Nov 2025. Meanwhile 2026 is the year GLP-1s went mass-market: pills and direct-from-manufacturer pricing.
- **Headline:** "The Weight-Loss Drug Didn't Slow Alzheimer's. Here's What the Belly-Fat Link Still Means."
- **Talking points:**
  1. **What the trials tested.** The drug was given to people *already diagnosed*. That says nothing about midlife prevention, which is where the risk is built.
  2. **The link that still holds.** Visceral fat drives the inflammation that raises cognitive risk, so losing the belly matters for the brain, by whatever honest means.
  3. **A GLP-1 is a tool, not a plan.** Protect muscle while the scale drops (protein first, strength training), and build the habits that outlast the prescription.
- **Do not say:**
  - that GLP-1s protect the brain;
  - prices or brand names;
  - anything that sounds like a prescribing recommendation.

  Use "If your physician prescribes it."
- **Outlet fit:** Star-Telegram health (Ciara McCarthy), DMN health care (Emily Brindley), Texas Tribune health, KERA News health, Houston Chronicle health, GLP-1 Hub podcast, menopause podcasts.

### Angle 5: A Benbrook author's plan to keep your mind (local human interest)
- **News hook:** Alzheimer's Awareness Month, a local author, and free community talks. Two more books are coming: *The Uninsured Decade* and *Count Yourself Skinny*.
- **Headline:** "After 30 Years in Practice, a Benbrook Author Is Teaching Fort Worth to Keep Its Mind."
  - The original working title, "A Fort Worth doctor's plan…", is softened on purpose. "Doctor" in a headline implies medical practice.
- **Talking points:**
  1. **The pattern he watched for 30 years:** the slow slide shows up long before anyone names it.
  2. **What he built:** a free program (assessment, book, Logbook, weekly Zooms) and free talks at libraries and service clubs.
  3. **The line that sticks:** "Don't lose your identity and your dignity while you still have a choice."
- **Outlet fit:** Fort Worth Report, Star-Telegram, Benbrook Life, Fort Worth Magazine, The Community News, Weatherford Democrat, Fort Worth Inc. (age-59 founder running an AI-operated company is a second hook), the A Time to Care podcast (Fort Worth).

> **Handle with care: the Push Patch (needle-free NAD+).**
>
> **Keep it out of every pitch.** If a host asks, TJ gives these four facts:
> 1. it is a prescription product, so a network physician decides who gets it;
> 2. the data comes from the manufacturer's internal studies and is not peer-reviewed;
> 3. no promises;
> 4. **TJ has a financial interest.** My4MLife sells it and TJ is the placing sales representative. He must disclose this on air if he mentions it at all.
>
> Health desks will treat an undisclosed product interest as disqualifying.

> **Media prep TJ needs before the first live interview:**
> 1. **His name on screen.** Reporters will want a full name for the lower-third and the byline: "Dr. TJ" alone, or "Dr. TJ Mundheim"? TJ decides. Pick one and use it everywhere.
> 2. **"What is your doctorate in?"** Answer honestly and briefly, then pivot to "I speak as a health-span educator."
> 3. **His history.** News reporters search a guest's history before booking. If anything about license history comes up, use only the prepared if-asked statement in the private legal folder. Never volunteer it, and never improvise.
> 4. **Talk about patterns, not patients.** Never "my patient", "I prescribed" or "I treat."

## 2. Target list, ranked by speed to booking

**How to read "speed":**
- **Days:** the outlet is actively asking for guests, or it is a self-serve platform.
- **1–3 wk:** a news desk or morning show with a published pitch route and a timely hook.
- **Months:** magazines (long lead times) and national outlets.

Routes are public only: the outlet's own forms, desk emails the outlet publishes, or the show's listing on a guest-matching directory. - WFAA Good Morning Texas and KPRC Houston Life both run items labeled "Sponsored".

Ask every lifestyle show up front whether a slot is editorial or sponsored, and **pay for nothing** without TJ's decision. TPR's The Source warns about scam "producers" who ask guests for money.
