## Section headers + 1-2 sentence summaries

### 1. The marketing problem with creatine
It got branded as a meathead supplement in the 1990s and never recovered. Meanwhile the cognitive literature quietly piled up to the point where, in 2024, a single 0.35g/kg acute dose was shown to reverse sleep-deprivation cognitive deficits in healthy adults (Gordji-Nejad et al. 2024).

### 2. The working-memory data (Rae 2003)
Rae et al. 2003 — randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind. Vegetarian adults given 5g/day creatine monohydrate for six weeks improved working memory and intelligence test performance vs. placebo. This is one of the cleanest cognitive RCTs in the supplement literature and almost nobody talks about it.

### 3. Sleep-deprived cognitive resilience (Gordji-Nejad 2024)
Acute creatine, single high dose, reversed cognitive decline during 21 hours of sleep deprivation. The mechanism: creatine buffers brain ATP when the system is energetically stressed. For a 55-year-old executive flying redeyes or sleeping six hours, this is not a small finding.

### 4. The aging brain runs low on phosphocreatine
Phosphocreatine declines with age. Brain creatine uptake is dose-dependent. Avgerinos et al. 2018 systematic review: cognitive benefit in older adults at 5g/day. Chilibeck et al. 2017 meta-analysis: lean mass plus cognitive function in adults 50+ on resistance training plus creatine.

### 5. What "premium nootropic" brands don't want you to know
A $4 tub of micronized creatine monohydrate has more RCT support for cognitive performance than every proprietary "focus blend" on the market combined. The brain-health-first frame says: start with what's evidenced, layer the rest only if there's reason.
