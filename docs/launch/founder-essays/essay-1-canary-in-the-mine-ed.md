## Section headers + 1-2 sentence summaries

### 1. What the prescription pad misses
The men's-health industry sells ED like it's a plumbing issue. It isn't. The penis is a barometer for vascular, hormonal, and neurological health — and a pill that masks the signal also wastes the warning.

### 2. The canary in the coal mine
Endothelial dysfunction shows up in the smallest vessels first. The penile arteries are roughly 1–2mm. Coronary arteries are 3–4mm. Cerebral arteries vary but the pattern is the same: small vessels fail first. ED is often the earliest clinical manifestation of a systemic vascular problem that will reach the heart and brain on a delay.

### 3. The L-citrulline data nobody quotes
Cormio et al. 2011 — randomized, placebo-controlled — 1.5g/day L-citrulline improved erection hardness scores in men with mild ED. Not as fast as a PDE5 inhibitor, but it works on the underlying nitric oxide pathway rather than rescuing one specific moment. The same NO pathway feeds cerebral perfusion.

### 4. Hormones, vascular, cognitive — one system
Testosterone supports endothelial function. Insulin resistance crushes both. Visceral fat drives the cytokine storm that damages vessels everywhere — including the blood-brain barrier. The man whose erection is failing at 52 is the same man whose word-finding will fail at 62 if nothing changes.

### 5. What to actually do
The 4M frame: mitigate (root-cause inflammation, glucose, sleep), move (training that drives nitric oxide and BDNF), motivate (the granddaughter's-wedding test), mind (the cognitive payoff is the real prize). ED is the doorway. The brain is the destination.
