## What the working-memory data actually shows

The cleanest cognitive trial on creatine ran in 2003. Rae and colleagues, published in *Proceedings of the Royal Society B*. Randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind. Adults given 5 grams a day of creatine monohydrate for six weeks. Outcome: significantly improved working memory and intelligence test performance vs. placebo.

The subjects were vegetarians, which is the design detail that makes the study elegant. Vegetarians have lower baseline brain creatine stores, so the effect size was larger. Subsequent studies in omnivores show a smaller but real effect.

## Sleep-deprived cognitive resilience

The newer finding is even more interesting for the demographic our team serves.

In 2024, Gordji-Nejad and colleagues published in *Scientific Reports* — a single 0.35-gram-per-kilogram dose of creatine, given before 21 hours of sleep deprivation, **reversed the cognitive decline normally seen under that condition**. The mechanism is straightforward. Under metabolic stress the brain burns through ATP faster than it can regenerate. Creatine acts as a phosphate buffer, donating to ADP to keep ATP available where it's needed.

If you are a man in your fifties or sixties flying redeyes, running a business, watching grandkids, or just doing the daily work of a long life — this is not a small finding. This is "your brain can be biochemically reinforced against the conditions you are already living under."

The premium-nootropic stack with no creatine in it has none of this evidence behind it. Most of those formulas have proprietary blends dosed-down on the actives so far that, even if the ingredients worked, you would not see a clinical effect at the dose delivered.

## The aging brain runs low on phosphocreatine

Brain phosphocreatine concentrations decline with age. This is documented across the imaging literature. The decline tracks loosely with the cognitive aging arc we all see — sharper at 35, foggier at 65, sometimes lost at 80.

Brain creatine uptake is dose-dependent. The Avgerinos 2018 systematic review found cognitive benefit in older adults at 5 grams a day. The Chilibeck 2017 meta-analysis showed creatine plus resistance training improved lean mass and cognitive function in adults over 50.
