## §5.5 — The 30-Day Recommit

You will fall off. Not *if*, *when*.

Three weeks in, life will intervene. Work travel. A family thing. A bad week of sleep that cascades into a bad week of food that cascades into a bad week of training. You will go three days without doing any of the four daily check-ins. You will skip the Sunday reflection. You will look at the scorecard on your bathroom mirror and feel something between guilt and resignation.

This is the moment that ends 90% of protocols.

It does not have to end this one.

What follows is a procedure. Not a pep talk. Not a re-motivation. A procedure for what to do when compliance breaks.

### Step 1 — Do Not Try to Make It Up

Do not double-stack supplements you missed. Do not skip meals to "balance" the bad week. Do not punish-lift Saturday morning because you missed Tuesday and Thursday. Do not write a long apologetic page in your weekly reflection.

The instinct to make up for lost time is what turns a one-week slip into a six-week absence. The men who try to recover heroically usually quit. The men who recover quietly usually stay.

### Step 2 — Pick One

Read your last Sunday reflection. Read the four daily check-ins (§5.1). Read the scorecard.

Pick ONE behavior. Just one. Not all four. Not even two.

The one you pick is whichever one is easiest *right now*. Not the one you "should" pick. Not the most important one. The one with the lowest activation energy this morning.

For most men returning from a slip, the answer is the morning sunlight walk. Eight minutes outside. That's it. That is the recommit move.

### Step 3 — Run That One for Three Days

Do the one behavior you picked. Just that one. Three days in a row. Today, tomorrow, the day after.

Do not touch the other three. Do not pretend you're "back on" the protocol. You're not. You're running one behavior for three days. That's the work.

### Step 4 — On Day Four, Add Back One More

After three consecutive days of the one behavior, add the second easiest. Run two behaviors for three more days.

### Step 5 — Continue Stacking Until You Are at Full Compliance

Three days at one. Three days at two. Three days at three. Three days at four. By day 13, you are running the full daily floor again. By day 14, the recommit is complete.

### Why This Works

Compliance is engineered, not willed.

When you try to "get back on track" by doing everything at once, you are asking the same willpower that just failed you to suddenly succeed. It won't. The architecture has to be rebuilt from one stable block at a time.

Three days of one behavior creates a foundation. Adding a second behavior on a stable foundation has roughly 4x the success rate of trying to start two behaviors at once.

This is the same principle that runs inside the My4MLife app. When the dashboard sees a slip, it does not ask the Protégé to do more — it asks for less, structured. The app is doing the same procedure this page is doing on paper.

### One More Rule

When you fall off — and you will — **do not stop showing up to the weekly Zoom.** Show up even when you have run zero behaviors that week. The cohort knows everyone falls off. The men who keep showing up at the Zoom during their slip weeks are the ones who recommit. The men who avoid the Zoom because they're embarrassed are the ones who disappear for six months.

Showing up at the Zoom IS a behavior. Add it to your three-day count.

### The Bigger Truth

This protocol is not the kind of thing you do for 30 days and finish.

It is the kind of thing you do for the rest of your life with periodic slips, periodic recommits, and gradual compounding that becomes visible in your face, your mind, your relationships, and your morning energy somewhere between months 6 and 18.

Begin with the end in mind. Re-begin every time you need to. The end stays the same.
