## §5.1 — Daily Check-Ins

Four behaviors, every day. If you do nothing else from this book, do these four. They are not separate items on a to-do list. They are one architecture, and they hand off to each other.

### 1. Morning Sun + Walk (8-15 minutes)

**What:** Get outside, in direct sunlight, within the first 30-60 minutes of waking. Walk while you do it. Do not wear sunglasses.

**Why:** This is the master circadian signal. Morning light through the eyes resets the suprachiasmatic nucleus — the brain's clock — which then sets cortisol, melatonin, and the entire downstream hormone cascade for the day. Skip this and every other circadian intervention is fighting upstream.

**How:** Walk to the end of the block. Walk to the coffee shop. Walk around your yard. Eight minutes is the floor. Fifteen is better. Through a window does not count. Through sunglasses does not count.

**Track:** ☐ Done · ☐ Skipped · ☐ Partial (through window / sunglasses)

---

### 2. Protein-First Breakfast (≥30g, opens the eating window at 9 AM)

**What:** When you break your overnight fast — default time is 9 AM — the first 30+ grams of food across your lips are lean protein. Not coffee with cream. Not toast and eggs. Protein first. Other macros wait 30 minutes.

**Why:** Amino acid availability primes muscle protein synthesis. Protein-first also produces a sharper satiety signal (PYY, GLP-1 endogenous) than fat-first or carb-first, which means less hunger across the day. And it stabilizes blood glucose at the moment your cortisol is naturally peaking, which means no morning crash.

**How:** Three eggs and a piece of grilled chicken from yesterday. A scoop of protein powder in water. A small steak. A turkey patty. Whatever it takes to hit 30g of lean protein before anything else. Then wait 30 minutes. Then add fat and carbs.

**Track:** ☐ ≥30g protein-first · ☐ <30g · ☐ Skipped breakfast · ☐ Ate other macros first

---

### 3. Eating Window Closes at 6 PM

**What:** No food after 6 PM. Water is fine. Herbal tea is fine. Food, no.

**Why:** Three reasons stacked. (a) Digestion competes with sleep architecture — late food fragments REM and reduces glymphatic clearance. (b) Eating in the evening signals "active" to the circadian system at the exact moment the body should be transitioning to rest. (c) Most men over 50 carry excess weight not because of total calories but because of evening grazing — closing the window at 6 closes a category of decision-making.

**How:** Eat dinner before 6. If you've got a business dinner that runs later, eat lighter and earlier in the day to compensate, and accept the bend. Don't let bend become break.

**Track:** ☐ Closed at 6 PM · ☐ Closed by 7 PM · ☐ Closed after 7 PM · ☐ Grazed past 9

---

### 4. Lights Out by 10:30 PM

**What:** Phone down, screens off, room dark, in bed by 10:30. Asleep by 11. Wake-up around 6 AM gives you a 7-hour sleep window.

**Why:** Glymphatic clearance — the brain's waste removal system — runs during deep NREM sleep, which clusters in the first half of the night. Going to bed at midnight cuts your glymphatic time in half regardless of how long you stay asleep. The men who sleep 8 hours but go to bed at 1 AM are not getting the brain-clearance benefit of 8 hours.

**How:** Backwards-plan. If lights out is 10:30, screens off is 9:30. If screens off is 9:30, last food was at 6. If last food was at 6, dinner started at 5:30. The chain runs upstream.

**Track:** ☐ Lights out by 10:30 · ☐ Lights out by 11:30 · ☐ Lights out after midnight

---

### The Daily Floor

These four are the daily floor. Sun, protein, eating-window, sleep. They are not negotiable, but they are also not heroic. None of them takes more than a few minutes of attention. They just take a few minutes *every day*.

Everything else in the protocol — the supplements, the lifts, the Mitigate work, the Motivate reflections — sits on top of these four. If the floor is unstable, the ceiling does not hold.

The four together take you about 25 minutes of intentional time on a normal day. That is the cost of admission.
