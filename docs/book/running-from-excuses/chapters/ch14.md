# Chapter 14

## The Road Ahead

If you've made it this far, I want to tell you something: I'm proud of you. It isn't the fact that you've finished another chapter that makes me say it. What makes me say it is that somewhere along the way, I believe you've probably started asking yourself different questions than the ones you walked in with. That's the goal of this whole book. Not agreement. Awareness.

When I began writing this book, I never wanted you to walk away trying to become another version of me. The world doesn't need another Randall; it already has one. What the world needs is the version of you that only you can become. That's why I've intentionally resisted telling you exactly what your life should look like. I can't answer that question for you. Only you can answer it. I don't know your story. I don't know your wounds. I don't know what you've survived, and I don't know what you're carrying with you today.

But I do know this much. Every one of us eventually arrives at the same intersection, and the roads that meet there are always the same. Excuses, or ownership. Reaction, or intention. Comfort, or growth. Those are the choices waiting for all of us. Nobody can choose that direction for you. Your spouse can't choose it. Your parents can't. Your coach can't, your pastor can't, and this book can't either. Eventually you're the one standing in front of the mirror. You're the one answering the questions. You're the one deciding who you're becoming. That is both the greatest responsibility and the greatest freedom any of us will ever receive in this life.

Through a long stretch of my life I wanted someone else to rescue me. I wanted someone else to fix my problems. I wanted someone else to tell me exactly what to do. Eventually the truth landed on me. God had already given me everything I needed to begin. Not every answer — just the next step. That's all He's ever really promised any of us: enough light for the next step, never the entire staircase all at once. Faith has always worked that way.

The remarkable thing is that the next step rarely feels extraordinary. Almost every time, it feels ordinary instead. It looks like making the phone call. It looks like having the conversation, taking the walk, reading the chapter. Apologizing to someone you've hurt. Lacing up your shoes. Turning off the television. Putting down the bottle. Telling yourself the truth. Ordinary decisions, repeated often enough over time, eventually become extraordinary lives.

That's exactly how my own life changed. There was no one massive breakthrough that solved everything; unremarkable faithfulness quietly compounded instead. Again. And again. And again. The same thing can happen for you, and it won't happen by making your story look like mine. It shouldn't look like mine. Your story will look like yours, and that's exactly the point. This book isn't asking you to copy my life. It's inviting you to honestly examine your own. Ask better questions. Tell yourself the truth. Keep today's promise. Then wake up tomorrow and repeat it all over again.

That's where becoming begins. It always has begun there, and it always will.

There is one thing I want to make absolutely clear before we go any farther. This book is not asking you to become me. I don't want that, and I mean it. My road is my road, and yours will be different. Your starting line will be different. Your obstacles will be different. Your gifts will be different. Your calling will be different too. If all you take away from these pages is that you should run every day, you've completely missed the point of the whole book. Running was never the answer. Running was only my vehicle. For someone else, the vehicle might be walking. It might be lifting weights. It might be learning a new skill, or rebuilding a marriage. It might be getting sober. It might be returning to church. It might be nothing more than making the one phone call you've been avoiding longer than you'd like to admit.

The action itself isn't what matters most. The commitment behind it does. That's why I keep returning to the same question: Who do I have to become? Not: What do I have to accomplish? Accomplishments eventually come to an end. Identity keeps growing.

One of the greatest mistakes I made through all those years was believing that success would finally be the thing that changed me. Now I know something very different. Becoming changes success, and not the other way around. The man I had to become in order to rebuild my life would have handled the restaurant differently. He would have handled pressure differently. He would have handled alcohol differently, and he would have handled relationships differently too. That's why becoming always comes first. Everything else grows from there.

If someone asked me today what the greatest lesson from this entire road has been, I don't think I'd talk about the ultramarathon. I don't think I'd talk about the running streak, or even the restaurant. I'd probably say this instead: the life you've always wanted is usually waiting on the other side of the person you're willing to become. That one sentence summarizes the whole path better than almost anything else I could write.

I spent year after year trying to change my circumstances. God spent those same years patiently changing me. Only now can I fully appreciate the difference between those two things. If my circumstances had changed before my character did, I probably would have repeated many of the same mistakes all over again. Instead, God changed the foundation first, and only then did He begin changing what rested upon it.

When I survey my own life from where I stand today, I can clearly see His fingerprints — not only in the victories, but in the valleys too. The abuse. The restaurant. The lawsuits. The depression. The rocking chair. The hotel room. The pandemic. The running. Every chapter of my life carried something forward, and none of it was wasted. That wasn't because every chapter was good. Plenty of them weren't. It's because God is. That's the difference.

I don't claim to understand every painful thing that's happened in my life. I probably never will understand it all. But I do know this: I've watched Him repeatedly create purpose from pain, hope from heartbreak, strength from weakness, and light from darkness. That's why I no longer ask, "Why me?" The better question has become, "God... who can I serve because I walked through this?"

That question changed everything for me. It shifted my focus away from my own wounds and toward someone else's healing.

As I've reflected on everything you've read so far, it has become clear to me that there are really only two ways to live. You can spend your life reacting, or you can spend your life preparing. I know both of those lives well, because I've lived both of them. I spent a long season reacting — reacting to childhood wounds, reacting to financial pressure, reacting to business failure, reacting to anxiety, reacting to alcohol, reacting to circumstances I believed were controlling me. Eventually I discovered something that completely changed my perspective: preparation is one of the greatest expressions of hope there is. People who prepare do it because they believe tomorrow is worth preparing for.

That may seem obvious. It wasn't obvious to me. When I believed my life wasn't going to change, I stopped preparing. I stopped investing in myself. I stopped believing the future deserved today's effort, and when all of that stopped, hope quietly disappeared from my life. The instant hope returned, preparation returned right along with it. I started reading again. I started training again. I started praying differently, planning differently, and thinking differently. Nobody forced any of it on me. It happened because I finally believed tomorrow could be different, and hope always changes today's decisions.

That's one of the reasons I believe excuses are so dangerous. Excuses steal hope. They convince us that nothing we do today really matters, when in fact the truth is exactly the opposite. Today's ordinary decisions are quietly building tomorrow's life, whether we recognize it or not. Every promise we keep, every conversation we have, every difficult decision we stop avoiding, every act of integrity — every single one of them becomes another brick in the foundation we're building.

The beautiful thing is that nobody else has to understand your foundation while you're building it. When I started running every day, many people probably thought I was just becoming healthier. They couldn't see what was actually happening beneath the surface. God was rebuilding my integrity. When I started asking, "Who do I have to become?" people probably assumed I was setting goals. They couldn't see what was actually happening there either. God was changing my identity. Most of the greatest work God does in our lives happens long before anyone else ever notices it. That's encouraging, because it means your progress doesn't have to be visible to be real.

So keep becoming. Keep planting. Keep preparing. Keep telling yourself the truth. One day someone will look at your life and think, "You're lucky." They'll see the harvest. What they won't see are the thousands of quiet mornings that produced it. That's okay. The harvest was never the goal in the first place. Faithfulness was.

Faithfulness has become one of my favorite words, because faithfulness is something every one of us can choose today. Not next year. Today. You don't have to run sixty-two miles today. You don't have to write a book. You don't have to transform your entire life before the sun goes down tonight. All you have to do is keep today's promise. That's enough for today. Then tomorrow, keep another one. One day you'll look around and understand something remarkable: you didn't merely accomplish more. You became someone different. And that's a life no accomplishment could ever replace. Becoming has never really been about reaching a destination. It's about faithfully walking the road God places in front of you, wherever that road happens to lead.

One ordinary step at a time.

One final thought before we move into the practical application of everything we've discussed. Please don't read this book looking for another motivational high. Motivation fades. I know that because I've chased it myself. There were whole seasons when I thought one more conference would finally change me. Or one more book. One more podcast. One more breakthrough. Then I'd return home, and life would look exactly the same as it did when I left. The problem was never inspiration. The problem was always implementation. Motivation gets you started. Habits keep you moving. Identity is what determines whether you continue.

That's why I no longer chase emotional highs. I chase plain faithfulness instead. There are still mornings I don't feel like running. There are days I don't feel like having difficult conversations. There are times I'd much rather choose comfort than growth. Those feelings don't surprise me anymore; they're simply part of being human. The difference today is that my feelings no longer get the final vote. My commitments get that vote now.

One of the greatest freedoms I've ever experienced is discovering that I don't have to negotiate with every emotion that enters my mind. Thoughts come. Feelings come. Excuses come. Not all of them deserve a response from me. Some deserve nothing more than to pass by without permission to stay. When that truth finally sank in, it changed my life, because I had spent so many years believing that every thought was true. Now I know better than that. Truth deserves agreement. Lies deserve replacement.

That's why Scripture has become so important to me. It isn't that reading a few verses magically solves life's problems. It's that truth slowly replaces lies. The lies I heard as a little boy. The lies I repeated to myself as an adult. The lies that eventually led me into that rocking chair. None of those lies disappeared overnight. They were replaced, one truth at a time and one decision at a time.

One ordinary day at a time.

If you're waiting until you feel different before you begin, you'll probably spend a very long time doing nothing but waiting. Begin anyway. Tell yourself the truth anyway. Keep today's promise anyway. The feelings eventually catch up to the life you're building. I've watched it happen too many times to believe anything otherwise. One day you'll wake up and understand something. The person you spent all those years hoping to become has quietly turned into the person looking back at you in the mirror each morning. And it won't be the result of one extraordinary breakthrough. It will be the result of thousands of small decisions that nobody else ever saw.

That's how becoming works. It works quietly. Faithfully. Patiently. One day at a time.

Now... let's make this personal.
