# Chapter 15

## Your Turn

For most of this book, you've been walking through my story. Now I want you to begin thinking about yours. Please understand — it isn't that my story is more important than yours, because it isn't. If anything, I hope my story has done nothing more than give you permission to look honestly at your own. That has been my goal from the very beginning. I never set out to convince you to become me. I set out to encourage you to become the person you were created to become.

Only you can answer that question. Only you know where you've been making excuses. Only you know which conversations you've been avoiding. Only you know where you've settled, and only you know what you've been pretending not to see. The remarkable thing is that you don't have to solve your entire life today. You just have to tell yourself the truth.

Truth has become one of my favorite words — not always a comfortable one, but always a freeing one. Excuses cannot survive in the presence of truth. The instant we honestly acknowledge where we are, we finally know where to begin.

For a long stretch of my life, I lied to myself. Not that I wanted to lie — the lies were just easier. "I don't have time." "I'll start Monday." "I work too much." "I deserve another drink." "I'll deal with it later." Those statements felt harmless. They weren't. Every one of them quietly delayed the life I actually wanted.

The hardest conversation you'll ever have may not be with another person. It may be the one you have with yourself. No distractions, no excuses, no one else to blame. Just truth. Where are you really? Not where you pretend to be, and not where everyone else thinks you are. Where are you? That question isn't meant to produce guilt. It's meant to produce clarity. Clarity creates direction. Direction creates momentum. Momentum changes lives.

Everything we've talked about in this book began with one honest admission.

I wasn't okay.

That sentence changed my life. It didn't solve my problems — it finally allowed me to begin solving them. You don't have to have every answer today. You don't have to know exactly what your life will look like five years from now, and you don't have to feel completely confident. You only have to become honest enough to take the next faithful step. That's all I did. One conversation. One decision. One run. One promise. Then another. Then another. Eventually, those everyday decisions became a different life.

They can become yours too. Not because you're extraordinary. Because ordinary faithfulness still works. It always has. It always will.

---

Before you turn the page, I want to ask you to do something I wish I had done years earlier. Don't answer these questions quickly, and don't answer them the way you hope they're true. Answer them honestly. Because honesty changes lives. Excuses protect comfort. Truth creates change.

So let me ask you a few questions. What story have you been telling yourself that isn't true anymore? What excuse has quietly become part of your identity? What conversation have you been avoiding? What promise have you continued postponing? What part of your life keeps demanding your attention while you continue pretending not to notice?

Nobody else has to answer those questions. You do. That's where becoming begins — not with another book, not with another coach, not with another motivational speech. With honesty.

One of the greatest mistakes I made was believing awareness alone created change. It doesn't. Awareness only reveals where the work begins. The work still has to be done. The conversation still has to happen. The apology still has to be made. The shoes still have to be laced up. The bottle still has to stay closed. The phone call still has to be made. Knowing isn't enough. Action changes lives.

I've often wondered how different my life would have been if someone had asked me these questions when I was eighteen years old. Or twenty-five. Or thirty. Would I still have opened the restaurant? Probably. Would I still have made mistakes? Absolutely — mistakes are part of becoming. But I don't think I would have spent nearly as many years running from myself.

That's why I wrote this book. It isn't that I have life figured out, because I don't. It isn't that I've stopped growing, because I haven't. I wrote it because I hope someone asks these questions sooner than I did. Maybe you're sixteen. Maybe you're sixty. It honestly doesn't matter. Today is still earlier than tomorrow, and one small decision made today changes every day that follows.

That's the beautiful thing about becoming. You don't have to rewrite your entire life this afternoon. You simply have to stop lying to yourself today. Then tomorrow, tell yourself the truth again. Keep another promise. Take another step. Growth rarely happens all at once. It quietly compounds, just like excuses once did. Only this time, you're compounding something much better: truth, integrity, hope, faithfulness. One ordinary day after another.

And one day you'll look back — not with regret, but with gratitude that you finally decided to begin.

---

One final encouragement before we leave this chapter. Please don't become overwhelmed. That's another trap. I've watched people become so discouraged by how far they have to go that they never take the first step. I've done it myself. The distance between where you are and where you want to be can feel enormous. Don't focus on the entire distance. Focus on today's step.

The same principle carried me through every difficult season of my life. The restaurant. The lawsuits. Quitting drinking. Rebuilding my health. The running streak. The ultramarathon. Writing this book. None of those things happened all at once. Every one of them happened exactly the same way. One decision. Then another. Then another.

Progress has a remarkable way of disguising itself as routine. While you're living it, it rarely feels dramatic. Then one day you look behind you and realize you've traveled much farther than you ever imagined. That's one of the reasons I no longer become discouraged by slow progress. Slow progress is still progress, and the direction matters far more than the speed.

I think we spend too much of our lives comparing our Chapter 2 to someone else's Chapter 20. Comparison has a way of stealing gratitude for the ground we've already covered. Don't do that. Your story is yours. Your pace is yours. Your calling is yours. The only life you've been asked to faithfully steward is your own. Run your race. Learn your lessons. Keep your promises. Become your person. One of the greatest gifts you can give yourself is permission to grow without constantly comparing your growth to someone else's. God has never compared my road to yours. I've wasted enough years doing that myself. Not anymore.

One lesson I've learned through running has become one of my favorite reminders. You don't finish sixty-two miles by thinking about mile sixty-two. You finish it by faithfully running mile one. Then mile two. Then mile three. Life works exactly the same way. Today's conversation deserves today's attention. Today's opportunity deserves today's effort. Today's promise deserves today's integrity. Tomorrow will eventually become today, and when it does, keep another promise. That's all becoming has ever asked of me. That's all it will ever ask of you.

Don't wait until you feel ready. Don't wait until life becomes easier. Don't wait until fear disappears.

Begin.

God has an incredible way of meeting us after we take the first faithful step. He certainly did for me, and I believe He'll do the same for you. Because the person you're capable of becoming may be much closer than you think. You just haven't met them yet.

---

As you work through the rest of this book, I want to encourage you to resist one temptation. Don't merely admire these ideas. Live them. I've spent year after year reading books that inspired me for a few days. Then life returned to normal, and nothing changed. The ideas weren't the problem — the problem was that I admired them more than I applied them. There's a tremendous difference. Admiration feels productive. Application actually is.

If one chapter challenges you to make one difficult phone call, make it. If one page reminds you to apologize, do it. If one question exposes an excuse you've been protecting, tell yourself the truth. Don't wait until you've finished the book. Begin today. The life you're hoping to build doesn't start after the last chapter. It starts after the next honest decision.

One thing I've noticed about excuses is that they're incredibly creative. If we're not careful, we'll even use personal growth as another excuse. "I'll start after I finish this book." "I'll begin after I read one more chapter." "I'll make changes once I know a little more." I smiled writing those words because I've said every one of them. Knowledge without action quietly becomes another hiding place. Don't let that happen. The goal isn't finishing another book. The goal is becoming another person.

Every chapter should leave you asking one simple question: What's one thing I can do today? Not ten. One. One conversation. One habit. One apology. One prayer. One promise. One act of courage. One decision. Then tomorrow, ask the same question again.

I've discovered something beautiful about everyday faithfulness. It removes the pressure of trying to change your entire life at once. God has never asked me to faithfully live next year. He's asked me to faithfully live today. That's enough. More than enough. Because enough faithful todays eventually become an entirely different life.

If there's one thing I hope you remember long after you've finished reading this book, it's this: Don't underestimate ordinary decisions. They may not feel significant while you're making them. Years later, you'll discover they quietly changed everything. That's exactly what happened to me. I kept today's promise. Then tomorrow's. Then the next. Eventually, the life I once hoped for slowly became the life I was living. Not because I was extraordinary. Because ordinary faithfulness still works. It always has. It always will.

Now let's take everything we've talked about and begin putting it into practice.
