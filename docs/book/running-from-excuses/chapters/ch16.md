# Chapter 16
## Close the Book. Open Your Life.

If you've read this far, thank you. Not because you finished my story, but because I hope you've started thinking differently about your own.

When I first sat down to write this book, I thought I was writing about running. Then I thought I was writing about excuses. Eventually it became clear to me that I was writing about becoming. Running was just the avenue. Excuses became the obstacle. Becoming became the destination.

If you remember nothing else from these pages, I hope you remember this. You are not your worst decision. You are not your greatest failure. You are not the lies someone else spoke over your life. You are not the mistakes you've made. You are not too late, and you are not too far gone.

I know those statements are true because I spent a long stretch of my life believing exactly the opposite. I believed the restaurant defined me. It didn't. I believed alcohol defined me. It didn't. I believed depression defined me. It didn't. I believed childhood trauma defined me. It didn't. Those became chapters — important chapters, painful chapters, necessary chapters. But they were never the ending. The ending was still being written.

So is yours.

That's one of the reasons I love books. No one judges a story by the middle. Only by the ending. Life deserves the same grace. Don't decide your future based on a chapter you're still living. Keep writing. Keep becoming. Keep moving.

One ordinary day at a time.

When I look back over my own life, I no longer see disconnected events. I see preparation. A grandmother teaching patience through a rose garden. A father teaching consistency through routine work. Abuse that taught me what lies sound like. A restaurant that exposed where my identity had been misplaced. A rocking chair that finally broke my pride. A hotel room that gave me hope. A pair of running shoes that taught me integrity. An ultramarathon that reminded me ordinary disciplines prepare us for extraordinary moments. Every chapter carried something into the next one. None of it was wasted — and it wasn't because every chapter was good. It's because God is.

That has become one of the greatest sources of peace in my life. He doesn't waste pain. He redeems it. Sometimes immediately, sometimes years later, and sometimes through another person whose life is changed because you were willing to tell the truth about yours. That's why I finally stopped asking, "Why did this happen?" I became much more interested in asking, "How can God use it?" That question continues changing my life. Maybe it'll begin changing yours too.

As you close this book, I want to ask you for one favor. Don't admire it. Use it. I've read too many books that made me feel inspired for a weekend. Then Monday came. Life got busy, the excitement faded, and nothing actually changed. I don't want that to happen here. If all this book gives you is another collection of highlighted quotes, I've failed. If it helps you become more present with your family, we've succeeded. If it gives you the courage to finally tell yourself the truth, we've succeeded. And if it encourages you to put down the bottle, make the phone call, start the walk, repair the relationship, forgive someone, or forgive yourself — then every page was worth writing.

I don't know what excuse you've been carrying. Maybe it's fear. Maybe it's shame. Maybe it's comfort. Maybe it's believing you're too old, or too young, or too broken, or too far behind. I've believed many of those same lies. They're still lies. The beautiful thing about truth is that it doesn't ask where you've been. It only asks one thing: What are you going to do next?

That's all any of us really have. The next decision. The next conversation. The next opportunity to become. You don't have to become the best version of yourself this afternoon. You just have to become a slightly better version than you were yesterday, then repeat it tomorrow. One ordinary day. One small promise. One quiet decision. That's how excuses lose their power. That's how integrity grows. That's how families change, and that's how generations change. One person decides to become. Then another. Then another.

If my story has taught me anything, it's this: God can do extraordinary things with ordinary people who are willing to keep showing up. I believe that with all my heart, because I've lived it.

I'm not writing these words as someone who has arrived. I'm writing them as someone who's still asking the same question every morning: Who do I have to become today? The answer changes. The question never does. I hope it becomes part of your life the way it has become part of mine — not because I asked you to, but because one morning you'll notice it quietly changing the way you think. The way you speak. The way you love. The way you lead. The way you live.

And one day, without even knowing exactly when it happened, you'll discover something remarkable. The person you spent years hoping to become has quietly become the person looking back at you in the mirror. Not perfect. Not finished. Still growing, still learning, still becoming. That's all any of us are really called to do. Keep becoming. Keep telling the truth. Keep your promises. Keep your light on. Someone you haven't even met yet may someday need the courage your story gives them. Don't hide it. Share it. Then trust God with the ripple. The rest has never really been up to us. It has been our privilege to faithfully show up. Again. And again. And again.

Close the book. Open your life.

That's really what I've been hoping you'd do from the very beginning. It isn't that I want you to remember my story. I want you to begin writing yours. If all this book ever becomes is something you finished reading, it won't accomplish what I prayed it would. But if one ordinary decision from these pages changes the direction of your life, then every difficult chapter I lived was worth it.

I don't know where you're beginning. Maybe you're standing in front of a mirror avoiding your own reflection. Maybe you're carrying wounds from childhood that still whisper lies into your life today. Maybe you're exhausted. Maybe you're successful by everyone else's standards while quietly falling apart on the inside. Maybe you're doing well and just wondering what's next. Wherever you are, start there. Don't wait until you have more confidence. Don't wait until life settles down. Don't wait until Monday. Don't wait until next year. Begin where your feet are. Tell yourself the truth. Keep today's promise. Then wake up tomorrow and keep another one.

You don't have to outrun your past. You don't have to erase it. You only have to stop allowing it to decide who you're becoming. The chapter behind you may explain you. It never has to define you.

As I finish writing these final pages, I find myself thinking about that little boy riding beside his dad in an old pickup. The teenager carrying pain onto a football field. The young husband chasing success in a restaurant. The father sitting in a rocking chair believing lies. The man lying awake in a hotel room wondering if life could ever be different. If I could sit across from every one of those versions of myself today, I wouldn't give them a lecture. I wouldn't hand them a plan. I'd lean across the table and say, "Keep going. This chapter isn't the end. God isn't finished with you yet."

That's what I hope these pages become for you. A reminder. Not that life will become easy, but that your future doesn't have to look like your past. The road ahead won't always be smooth. You'll still make mistakes. You'll still have difficult days. You'll still need to make course corrections. So will I. Because becoming never ends.

That's actually good news. It means tomorrow is another opportunity. Another chance to tell yourself the truth. Another chance to keep your word. Another chance to become.

Thank you for trusting me with a small part of your journey. I hope our paths cross someday. If they do, I probably won't ask how many books you've read, or how many races you've finished, or how successful you've become. I'll probably ask one question.

Who are you becoming?

I hope you'll smile before you answer, because you'll know that question has no finish line. Neither does this road.

Now, go live your story. And when you have the opportunity, help someone else believe they can live theirs too.

## A Final Word

If you've made it to this page, thank you. Thank you for trusting me with part of your journey. Writing this book has forced me to revisit some of the most painful chapters of my life. It also reminded me of something I'll never forget. Healing doesn't come from pretending the past never happened. Healing comes from telling the truth about it — and choosing not to let it write the rest of your story.

I don't know what chapter you're living today. Maybe you're standing at a starting line. Maybe you're carrying more weight than anyone around you realizes. Maybe you're rebuilding. Maybe you're thriving. Maybe you're just trying to make it through another day. Wherever you are, I hope you remember this. You are not behind because your story has been difficult. You are not disqualified because you've made mistakes. You are not finished because you've failed. As long as you're still breathing, there is still another decision to make. Another promise to keep. Another opportunity to become.

My prayer is that years from now, you won't remember every story in this book. I hope you'll remember the question. The question that quietly changed my life.

Who do I have to become today?

Ask it when life is good. Ask it when life falls apart. Ask it when you're celebrating. Ask it when you're discouraged. Keep asking it, because the answer will continue changing as you grow. That's the beauty of becoming. It never ends.

If our paths never cross, I hope you'll know this: I'm cheering for you. Not because I know your story, but because I know what it's like to believe another chapter isn't possible.

It is.

Keep telling yourself the truth. Keep your word. Keep showing up. Keep your light on. And whenever you have the opportunity, help someone else believe that another chapter is still waiting to be written.

I'll see you somewhere down the road.
