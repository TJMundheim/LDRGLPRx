## 2. Two science claims (not changed)

**2a. Weighing weekly vs daily.**
Current, Ch 12: "**Weigh weekly, not daily.** Same scale, same morning, once a week. Daily weight is noise; weekly weight is a trend line. Trust the trend."
Current, Science, "The streak": "And the weekly (not daily) weigh-in matches trial evidence that regular self-weighing improves outcomes while daily weighing amplifies noise-driven discouragement."
Problem: the best-known trial (Steinberg 2015) favored daily weighing, so "matches trial evidence" is not supported.
Proposed Ch 12 (soften, keep the advice): "**Weigh weekly, not daily.** Same scale, same morning, once a week. Daily weight bounces with water and salt; weekly weight shows the trend. Some people do well weighing daily. If a daily number discourages you, weekly is the calmer way to watch the same line."
Proposed Science sentence: "Research consistently links regular self-weighing with better outcomes. Weekly is enough for the trend and kinder for people who find daily swings discouraging."
Apply the softer wording? **Recommend YES.**

**2b. Morning light and falling asleep faster.**
Current, Ch 5: "Studies show people who get their morning light fall asleep faster at night — up to half an hour faster — and sleep longer."
Current, Science: "...moves sleep onset earlier and lengthens sleep — on the order of falling asleep 20–30 minutes faster."
Problem: the supporting studies show a smaller, variable effect.
Proposed Ch 5: "Studies suggest people who get their morning light tend to fall asleep earlier at night, and some sleep longer."
Proposed Science: "Intervention studies show that shifting light exposure earlier tends to move sleep onset earlier and can lengthen sleep, though the size of the effect varies from person to person."
Apply the softer wording? **Recommend YES.**
