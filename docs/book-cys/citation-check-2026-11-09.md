# Count Yourself Skinny: citation check, 2026-11-09

Checked against `docs/book-cys/draft/_MASTER.md` (not edited). Line numbers refer to that file. Sources page = lines 959-972, items numbered 1-12 in order.

## Plain-English summary

**Good news:** all 12 papers are real, and the citation details (authors, journal, year, volume, pages) are correct as printed. I found no invented studies. Only trivial tidy-ups: item 3's pages should end 1212-1221.e3, and item 6 may add "Jr" after Wright KP.

**The problem is not the citations, it is what the book says the papers found.** Of the 12:

- **3 confirmed** (Ohkuma, Tudor-Locke and Bassett, Taheri), each with a small wording note.
- **9 need fixing.** The paper is real, but the book overstates it, uses it for something it did not study, or leaves out a limit a reporter would spot.

**Most serious problem:** the weigh-in advice. The book (lines 293, 347, 953) says "weigh weekly, not daily" and claims trial evidence that daily weighing causes discouragement. The best evidence runs the other way. A 2015 study by Steinberg (not on your Sources page) found that daily weighers lost more weight, and a 2014 randomized trial by the same group found daily weighing did not cause psychological harm. A 2015 review of 17 studies agreed. The claim has to be reworded or cut.

**Other serious ones:**
- The fasting claim (line 933) leans on a study of 8 men that was designed to keep their weight steady, so it cannot show weight loss. A larger 12-week trial of a 16:8 schedule found no meaningful difference from regular meals.
- "Up to half an hour faster" falling asleep (line 184, and "20-30 minutes" at line 941) could not be found in any source I could open. The cited light study is an observational study of office workers, not an intervention, and the Science section calls it an intervention.
- The chewing result (Iowa State) is one lunch, 45 young adults, in a lab. A matching study in older adults found no change in meal size. The book never tells the reader this.
- "A single bad night" of sleep (lines 199, 945): the study restricted sleep for two nights, in 12 young men.
- The water trial (line 949) was a diet trial in 48 adults aged 55-75, and both groups were also eating less. It was not water alone.

**Caveat on my own work:** the full text of Lally 2010 (66 days) and Figueiro 2017 was blocked (HTTP 403 and a bot check), so the exact minutes in Figueiro and the paper's own wording in Lally rest on PubMed/Crossref records plus secondary summaries. Those are flagged below.

### Known problems: independent verdicts

**(a) Weekly versus daily weighing: CONFIRMED as a problem. The book's evidence claim is wrong.**
- Steinberg 2015, J Acad Nutr Diet 115(4):511-518, PMID 25683820, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25683820/. Title: "Weighing every day matters." Within a 6-month trial of 47 adults using a daily-weighing programme, people who weighed every day lost about 6 kg more than those who weighed less than daily (those averaged 5.4 days a week), and adopted more weight-control behaviours. Limits: small, a comparison within one group rather than a randomized test of daily versus weekly, and the less-than-daily group still weighed most days. It does not test weekly weighing, and it is not on your Sources page.
- Steinberg 2014, Am J Prev Med 46(1):24-29, PMID 24355668, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24355668/. Randomized trial, 91 adults, 6 months: daily weighing caused no increase in depression, binge eating, or other measures, and body dissatisfaction and restraint scores improved.
- Zheng 2015, Obesity 23(2):256-265, PMID 25521523, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25521523/. Review of 17 studies: regular self-weighing was linked to more weight loss and not to harm.
- Book text to fix: L953 ("weekly (not daily) weigh-in matches trial evidence that regular self-weighing improves outcomes while daily weighing amplifies noise-driven discouragement"), L293 ("Weigh weekly, not daily... Daily weight is noise"), L347, L332, and Week 13 (the "smoke alarm" line). Suggested L953: "Studies of people trying to lose weight link regular self-weighing with better results and have not found it harms mood. Daily numbers bounce around with water and food, so judge yourself by the weekly trend." If the author wants to keep a weekly rule as a design choice (it is simpler and avoids fixating), say so as the author's choice, not as research.

**(b) "Up to half an hour faster": CANNOT CONFIRM.** See row 7. Neither Figueiro (observational, no minutes in the abstract) nor Wright (clock timing in 8 campers) supports it, and the Science section wrongly calls it an "intervention" result.

**(c) Iowa State chewing: CONFIRMED as described.** Zhu and Hollis 2014: 45 adults aged 18-45, three pizza lunches in a lab, crossover. The older-adult follow-up (Zhu and Hollis, Physiol Behav 2014, 18 adults, mean 72) found no difference in intake. See rows 1 and 1b.
