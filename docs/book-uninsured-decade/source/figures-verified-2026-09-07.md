## PART 2 — New Research

### 2a. The Long-Term-Care Insurance Market Collapse

- **Carrier collapse:** Of 100+ carriers selling standalone LTC insurance around 2000, fewer than a dozen actively sell new standalone policies today. [actuary.org](https://actuary.org/article/writing-long-term-care-in-a-short-term-world/)
- **Rate hikes (real examples):** Genworth — 68.5% average approved increase in a 2026 Nebraska filing; in 2022, raised rates for 2,000+ policyholders by an average of 97% (range 79–173%); a 161% request was denied in Massachusetts as "unjust, unfair and inequitable." John Hancock — approved increases in 10+ states in a single quarter (2026 filings, adding $35.1M in annual premium); prior cumulative hikes include 15% (2012–15), 32.3% (2017), 43.8% phased over 3 years (2020), and another 15% on some policies in 2024. [spglobal.com](https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/latest-news-headlines/genworth-john-hancock-lead-pack-on-long-term-care-rate-increases-in-q3-61264522), [longtermcaredesk.com](https://longtermcaredesk.com/rate-tracker/genworth/)
- **Typical premium, healthy 60-year-old couple today:** roughly **$4,600–$7,200/yr combined** for a moderate benefit ($165K initial benefit, 3% growth), per July 2026 pricing data; lower-end bundled quotes as low as $2,550–$4,675/yr for reduced coverage. [theseniorlist.com](https://www.theseniorlist.com/insurance/long-term-care/cost/)
- **Ownership rate:** Only **10–15% of Americans 65+** hold LTC insurance (estimates range 10% per Arctos Foundation/HCG Secure survey to 15% per Center for Retirement Research); only 3–4% of those 50+ pay LTC premiums currently — despite ~70% of 65+ eventually needing long-term care. [crr.bc.edu](https://crr.bc.edu/do-older-adults-understand-healthcare-risks/), [mcknightsseniorliving.com](https://www.mcknightsseniorliving.com/news/survey-only-10-percent-of-americans-have-long-term-care-coverage/)
- **Hybrid life/LTC growth:** LIMRA/EY reporting confirms fixed hybrid life-with-LTC products grew in 2025 and are described as the leading 2026 product trend, as standalone LTC insurers exit the market; 63% of consumers surveyed say they want LTC-linked coverage. [limra.com](https://www.limra.com/en/research/research-abstracts-public/2026/hybrid-insurance-on-the-rise-a-new-era-for-long-term-care-protection/)
- **Medicare exact wording:** "Medicare doesn't cover custodial care if it's the only care you need" (medicare.gov/coverage/long-term-care); statutory basis 42 CFR §411.400 excludes payment for custodial care.
- **Medicaid spend-down (Texas specifics, 2026):** Countable-resource limit = **$2,000 individual / $3,000 couple** (community spouse of an institutionalized applicant tested at the $2,000 limit); **5-year (60-month) look-back** on asset transfers, triggering a penalty period for gifts within that window; **Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA)** protects **$32,532–$162,660** for the at-home spouse, plus up to **$4,066.50/mo** in protected income (2026 figures); Texas is an income-cap state using a Qualified Income (Miller) Trust for applicants over the income limit. [dallaselderlawyer.com](https://dallaselderlawyer.com/2026-medicaid-spousal-resource-limits-texas/), [elderoptionsoftexas.com](https://www.elderoptionsoftexas.com/article-medicaid-spend-down.htm)

### 2c. Risk-Reduction Evidence (state conservatively)

- **Lancet Commission 2024** (14 modifiable risk factors: low education, hearing loss, hypertension, smoking, obesity, depression, physical inactivity, diabetes, excess alcohol, TBI, air pollution, social isolation, high LDL, untreated vision loss): eliminating all 14 could theoretically prevent/delay **~45%** of dementia cases worldwide (population-attributable fraction, not an individual guarantee). [alzheimer-europe.org](https://www.alzheimer-europe.org/news/2024-lancet-commission-underscores-potential-dementia-risk-reduction-identifying-14-modifiable?language_content_entity=en)
- **FINGER trial:** the original 2015 Finnish trial (foundational to the 2025 US POINTER design) showed a 2-year multidomain lifestyle intervention improved/maintained global cognition versus control — cited widely as the basis for the more rigorous US POINTER replication.
- **US POINTER (JAMA, July 28 2025):** 2,111 participants, mean age 68.2, 2-year RCT. Both structured and self-guided multidomain lifestyle interventions **improved cognition**; the structured arm showed **significantly greater improvement in global cognition** than self-guided, with benefit consistent across age, sex, ethnicity, cardiovascular status, and APOE-ε4 genotype. This is an improvement/protection-from-decline finding, not a "prevents dementia" finding. [jamanetwork.com](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2837046)
- **Physical activity meta-analyses:** pooled relative risk for all-cause dementia with higher physical activity ≈ **0.80** (95% CI 0.77–0.84), i.e., ~20% risk reduction; Alzheimer's-specific RR ≈ 0.86 (~14% reduction); vascular dementia RR ≈ 0.79 (~21% reduction). Leisure-time activity specifically: RR 0.76 (~24% reduction); note occupational physical activity showed a slightly *increased* risk (RR 1.20) in one large analysis — the benefit is domain-specific.
- **Type 2 diabetes / midlife obesity hazard ratios:** diabetes diagnosed at 60–69 → HR 1.70 for dementia; at 50–59 → HR 1.72; before 50 → HR 1.90 (vs. diagnosis ≥70); obese + diabetes diagnosed before 50 → HR **3.05** vs. non-obese diagnosed ≥50. Midlife metabolically unhealthy obesity in women → HR 1.62. [ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11559992](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11559992/)
- **What can honestly be claimed:** "Structured lifestyle change has been shown in randomized trials (US POINTER, JAMA 2025) to improve cognitive performance and protect against age-related decline over 2 years," and "up to an estimated 45% of dementia cases are linked to modifiable risk factors" (population level, not a personal guarantee).
- **What cannot be claimed:** that any lifestyle program, supplement, or peptide "prevents," "cures," "treats," or "reverses" Alzheimer's, dementia, or Parkinson's. This matches the existing compliance note in the source document and should stay unchanged.
- **PAF vs. individual risk — do not conflate these.** The Lancet Commission's 45% is a *population-attributable fraction*: the share of dementia cases that would theoretically disappear if the entire population eliminated all 14 modifiable risk factors simultaneously, from birth-cohort level. It is not a statement that any one person who "does the work" cuts their own personal risk by 45% — individual relative-risk-reduction evidence (US POINTER, physical-activity meta-analyses) supports numbers well below that, in the 20–30% range for a single domain like physical activity, with no RCT yet showing a multi-domain personal risk reduction anywhere near the population ceiling. **Use 45% only as a labeled ceiling ("if everything, for everyone, were addressed") and keep 30% as the conservative default for any individual relative-risk-reduction claim.** This distinction governs both the book copy and the §2d math below — the 30% and 45% columns in the table are not two equally valid personal estimates; 30% is the honest working assumption, 45% is shown only to demonstrate where the ceiling sits.

### 2d. Expected-Value Worked Example

**Assumptions (clearly stated, corrected 2026-09-08):**
- **Lifetime risk of dementia after age 55**, per Fang M, Coresh J, et al., "Lifetime risk and projected burden of dementia," *Nature Medicine*, published 13 Jan 2025 (ARIC cohort, >15,000 people followed dementia-free from age 55): **42% overall** (95% CI 41–43%), **35% for men**, **48% for women**; risk is higher in APOE ε4 carriers and Black adults (~45–60%). Projected new U.S. cases/year rise from 514,000 (2020) to roughly 1 million (2060). [nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03340-9](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03340-9); [PubMed 39806070](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39806070/); [NIH Research Matters summary](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/risk-future-burden-dementia-united-states).
- **For a mixed-sex couple**, P(at least one partner develops dementia) = 1 − P(neither does) = 1 − (1 − 0.35)(1 − 0.48) = 1 − (0.65 × 0.52) = 1 − 0.338 = **0.662 ≈ 66%**.
- **The superseded 15% figure** (an illustrative blend of the old Alzheimer's Association 1-in-5-women/1-in-10-men *Alzheimer's-only* lifetime-risk statistic) is carried in the table below **for the record only, marked superseded** — it materially understated the risk and should not be used going forward.
- **Caveat to state every time this number is used:** the 42%/66% figures are *all-cause dementia* risk, including mild late-life cases — a broader category than "Alzheimer's requiring 24/7 memory care." The $405,262 lifetime-cost figure applies specifically to those who go on to develop dementia and require care; it is not adjusted for case severity. Do not imply that 42%/66% of people face the full $405K decade — that number is the average cost *conditional on* developing the disease, not a further-discounted figure for mild cases.
- Average lifetime cost of care if dementia occurs: **$405,262** (Alzheimer's Association 2024 dollars, confirmed above).
- Individual relative risk reduction from sustained structured lifestyle intervention: **30%** conservative default (see PAF-vs-individual note in §2c). **45%** shown only as the Lancet Commission's population-level ceiling — labeled as such, never presented as an individual's expected reduction.
- Premium: **$10,000/yr for 10 years = $100,000 total spend.**

**Math (exact arithmetic):**

| Household | Lifetime risk | Unmitigated expected cost (risk × $405,262) | Expected savings at 30% RRR (risk × 0.30 × $405,262) | Expected savings at 45% ceiling (risk × 0.45 × $405,262) | Net vs. $100K premium — 30% | Net vs. $100K premium — 45% ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Single** (35% men / 48% women, use the individual's own figure) | 42% | 0.42 × $405,262 = **$170,210** | 0.42 × 0.30 × $405,262 = **$51,063** | 0.42 × 0.45 × $405,262 = **$76,595** | $51,063 − $100,000 = **−$48,937** | $76,595 − $100,000 = **−$23,405** |
| **Couple** ("does this happen to either of us") | 66.2% | 0.662 × $405,262 = **$268,283** | 0.662 × 0.30 × $405,262 = **$80,485** | 0.662 × 0.45 × $405,262 = **$120,728** | $80,485 − $100,000 = **−$19,515** | $120,728 − $100,000 = **+$20,728** |
| **Old 15% blend — superseded, for the record only** | 15% | 0.15 × $405,262 = **$60,789** | 0.15 × 0.30 × $405,262 = **$18,236** | 0.15 × 0.45 × $405,262 = **$27,355** | $18,236 − $100,000 = **−$81,764** | $27,355 − $100,000 = **−$72,645** |

**Reading it plainly:** the corrected 42%/66% figures move the expected-value case substantially closer than the old 15% blend did, but the honest reading does not change in kind, only in degree. At the conservative 30% individual relative-risk-reduction assumption — the number actually supported by trial evidence — a couple's expected dementia-cost savings (~$80,485) still fall short of the $100,000 ten-year premium by about $19,500: close, meaningfully closer than before, but still not a break-even on dementia-cost avoidance alone. Only at the Lancet Commission's 45% *population* ceiling — which is not a legitimate estimate of what any one household should expect — does the couple's number cross $100,000, and that crossing should never be sold as an individual result. The honest case for the premium spend remains what it was: (a) the same lifestyle factors compounding across cardiovascular, diabetes, and other disease risk simultaneously, well beyond this single line item, (b) the $405,262 figure being an average with a wide right tail — late-stage 24/7 care cases running well past $500K, as in the source document's own 10-year scenario, and (c) quality-of-life and caregiver-hour value not captured in dollar terms at all. State this transparently rather than implying a false break-even — a financially literate reader will do this math themselves, and now the corrected numbers hold up better under that scrutiny than the old ones did.
