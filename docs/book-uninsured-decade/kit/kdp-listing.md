## Description (paste into KDP; plain HTML)

```html
<p><b>Which policy in your folder pays for memory care?</b></p>
<p>You spent thirty years building coverage so that nothing could land on your family as a bill. The homeowner's. The umbrella. The term life you hope never pays. Medicare doesn't cover custodial care if it's the only care you need. Where a policy or hybrid rider fits your plan, it funds part of the risk. No contract lowers the odds.</p>
<p><i>The Uninsured Decade</i> is a book about money before it is a book about health. It is written to whoever holds the pen: the person who reads the statements, keeps the documents, knows the advisor by first name, and may one day make every decision alone.</p>
<p><b>Inside you will find:</b></p>
<ul>
<li><b>The bill.</b> A ten-year ledger for one household, priced line by line, with a source and a date on the figures.</li>
<li><b>The terrain.</b> What Medicare and Medicaid do and do not pay, how the long-term-care market thinned out, and why your advisor's review matters more now, not less.</li>
<li><b>The second patient.</b> What the decade costs the spouse who is not diagnosed.</li>
<li><b>The odds, stated as odds.</b> About 42% lifetime risk after 55 and about two in three for a couple, counting all dementia, including mild late-life cases (<i>Nature Medicine</i>, 2025). The arithmetic is on the page, including the part where this does not break even on dementia costs alone.</li>
<li><b>The premium.</b> Roughly ten net-new hours a month, and what those hours pay while you are alive to collect it.</li>
<li><b>The first thirty days.</b> A plan that ends with an hour across the table from your advisor, attorney, or CPA.</li>
</ul>
<p>Your professionals fund and structure the risk. This book is about the other half of the ledger. Some households should own both, so ask your advisor. If you do not have one, this book will help you find the right questions for the first meeting.</p>
<p>Nothing in this book prevents, treats, cures, or reverses dementia. The work it describes may lower the odds, and that is all an honest person can tell you.</p>
<p><i>Don't lose your identity and your dignity while you still have a choice.</i></p>
<p>Educational only. Not medical, legal, tax, insurance, or financial advice. Dr. TJ is a health-span educator with more than thirty years in clinical practice. He does not provide medical care through this book.</p>
```

## Price options: paperback

**One correction to the brief.** "60% of list minus print" is true only at $9.99 and up. KDP pays **50%** at $9.98 or below on Amazon.com, and **40%** minus print on Expanded Distribution **(KDP, checked)**.

**Print cost: $2.30 to confirm.** KDP's table lists a flat $2.30 for black-ink, regular-trim books of 24 to 110 pages on Amazon.com **(KDP, checked 2026-11-04)**. At 90 pages the page count does not change it. **TO CONFIRM:** enter the final page count in KDP's calculator at setup, because the rate can change. At 111 pages and up the formula becomes $1.00 + $0.012 per page.

| List price | Rate | Amazon.com royalty per copy | Expanded Distribution per copy (40%) | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $9.98 | 50% | (0.50 x 9.98) - 2.30 = **$2.69** | $1.69 | Avoid: one cent less than $9.99 costs a dollar a copy |
| $9.99 | 60% | (0.60 x 9.99) - 2.30 = **$3.69** | $1.70 | Lowest price at 60%. Reads as a pamphlet to this buyer |
| **$12.99** | 60% | (0.60 x 12.99) - 2.30 = **$5.49** | $2.90 | **My pick.** Fair for about 90 pages; well under the flagship's $16.99 at 320 pages |
| $14.99 | 60% | (0.60 x 14.99) - 2.30 = **$6.69** | $3.70 | Defensible: it is a money book for people who pay advisors |
| $16.99 | 60% | (0.60 x 16.99) - 2.30 = **$7.89** | $4.50 | Same price as a book 3.5 times longer. Invites a bad review |

The price is **TJ's call**. Three copies a week at $12.99 versus $14.99 is $3.60 a week, so choose on what a reader thinks is fair for 90 pages. Most copies in this plan move through advisors and dinners at author cost anyway.

**Author copies** cost the print price plus shipping, with no royalty. The September memo's "about $4 a copy" is plausible but unconfirmed. For anyone gifting under Texas insurance rules, the strategy notes a $25 limit on educational items, for counsel to confirm.
