## Locked 2026-09-08 (2)
- Corrected lifetime-risk basis: the old illustrative ~15% blended household dementia risk (built from the Alzheimer's-only 1-in-5-women/1-in-10-men figure) is **superseded**. Current basis is Fang M, Coresh J, et al., "Lifetime risk and projected burden of dementia," *Nature Medicine*, 13 Jan 2025 (ARIC cohort, >15,000 followed from age 55): lifetime risk of dementia after 55 = **42%** overall (35% men / 48% women); for a mixed-sex couple, P(at least one) = 1 − (0.65 × 0.52) ≈ **66%**. Applied across figures-verified-2026-09-07.md §2d, `_MASTER.md` (Ch1), and all launch collateral (advisor leave-behind, dinner-guest script, talk spec). Caveat carried everywhere it's used: this is all-cause dementia including mild late-life cases; the $405,262 lifetime-cost figure applies to those who go on to develop it.
- **PAF-vs-individual rule:** the Lancet Commission's 45% ("up to 45% of dementia is attributable to 14 modifiable risk factors") is a population-attributable fraction — the theoretical population-wide reduction if everyone eliminated every factor — not an individual's expected risk reduction. Never present 45% as a personal number. **30% stays the conservative default for any individual relative-risk-reduction claim**; 45% may appear only labeled explicitly as the population ceiling.
