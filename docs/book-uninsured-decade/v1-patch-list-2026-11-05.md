## E. Numbers, facts, and contradictions found in the read

### E1. The couple row in the Ch 7 table simplifies in two directions. TJ decides
**Status:** Printed arithmetic is correct. The method has a gap a planner will test.

**Now (line 403):** "| **Couple** — \"does this happen to either of us\" | 66.2% | $268,283 | **$80,485** | **−$19,515** |"

The table multiplies the chance of *at least one* case by *one* bill. It also applies a 30% cut directly to the 66.2%. Running it other ways:

| Method | Expected savings | Against $100,000 |
|---|---|---|
| Table as printed: 66.2% x 30% x $405,262 | $80,485 | **-$19,515** |
| Both spouses each cut risk 30% (35% to 24.5%, 48% to 33.6%). Chance of at least one falls from 66.2% to 49.9%, a 24.7% drop, with one bill | $66,187 | **-$33,813** |
| Count a second bill when both develop it. Expected cases 0.35 + 0.48 = 0.83 | $100,910 | **+$910** |

The third line means line 411, "It is the only version of this arithmetic that produces a positive number," is not strictly true. The premium is also understated in that version, because the $10,000 basket holds roughly one person's gym and trainer, so two people pay more. The honest conclusion holds, but the book should say why. The 66% also assumes the two risks are independent, which line 392 does not state.

**Proposed (add after line 405; the table stays as is):** "One note on the couple line, because a careful reader will run it. It assumes your two risks are independent and counts one bill per couple. Count a second bill in the households where both of you develop it, and the expected savings rise to about $100,900, roughly the premium. But two people running this protocol also pay more than the $10,000 here, which is closer to one person's gym and trainer. Run it either way and it is a close call on dementia costs alone, not a return."

**And (line 411):**

**Now (line 411):** "It is the only version of this arithmetic that produces a positive number, and I am not going to sell you the positive one."

**Change to:** "It is the version of this arithmetic that most clearly produces a positive number, and I am not going to sell you the positive one."

**My recommendation:** add the note. It strengthens the spine, and it is much better if the book says this first. A v2 co-author should re-run and sign this table.
