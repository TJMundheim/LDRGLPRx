## The new passage (Ch1, new h2 "Who holds the pen", after the inventory/odds section and "Self-pay, then spouse-pay, then impoverishment", before "Bill")

> ## Who holds the pen
>
> One more thing about that folder, because I would be lying to you by omission if I left it out. I am a man, and I write from that chair. But when I look at the households who actually do this work — and when I listen to the estate attorneys who sit across the table from them — the person holding the folder is very often not the husband. It is the wife, or the partner. She keeps the documents. She knows the advisor by first name. She is the one who signs the forms. And in a great many households she ends up making every one of these decisions alone, because her husband is the one whose mind is going, and he can no longer sit at that table with her.
>
> She is not only the one deciding. She carries the higher lifetime risk herself — 48% against his 35%, from the same study.
>
> So if that is you, this book was not written to your husband with you copied on it. It is written to whoever holds the pen.

182 words. It cites no new statistic. 48% and 35% are the existing Fang et al. (Nat. Med. 2025) figures from the paragraph just above it, which is the "same study". The claim about who decides is framed as what Dr. TJ and estate attorneys see, not as a percentage.

## Deliberately left sex-specific or unchanged

- **Dr. TJ's first-person voice and life details:** "I am fifty-nine…" (Ch10), "I am a man, and I write from my own chair" (front matter and Ch1), and the author disclaimer and About the Author ("he"). He is a man, and this is his voice.
- **Statistics, word for word:** 42% overall / 35% men / 48% women (Ch1, Ch7, Sources). The couple figure of ~66% = 1 − (0.65 × 0.52). Roughly two-thirds of Alzheimer's cases are in women (Ch10). The 63% caregiver mortality figure (Schulz & Beach). Every number and citation in Ch7's table is unchanged.
- **Ch10 "For the man reading this" / "For the woman reading this":** both canaries stay sex-specific, because the physiology differs (penile vs. coronary artery size; the perimenopausal cluster). The only addition is the sentence noted in row 16.
- **Ch9 visceral fat:** "in a man it runs an enzyme that converts his own testosterone into estrogen". This is male physiology, so it stays.
- **Bill & Cindy's sexes and every number attached to them:** Bill is the patient and Cindy the caregiver, kept that way because the same couple runs through the dinner-talk deck and keeping them identical keeps book and talk in sync. The rebalance gives Cindy the decider's job (folder, advisor, attorney, sole signature) without changing who gets sick. Ages, dates, and dollar figures are all unchanged.
- **"A word to the spouse" (front matter):** already neutral. Kept as written.
- **Ch2 "the one who sat across from your father":** a historical detail about an earlier generation of advisors, so it was left alone.
- **Inventory row "Your partner dies, business at risk":** "partner" here means a business partner.
