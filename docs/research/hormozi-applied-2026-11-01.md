## (a) The framework on one page

**The thesis across his three books.** Make an offer so good it feels stupid to say no (*$100M Offers*, 2021). Get it in front of enough people (*$100M Leads*, 2023). Sequence offers so a customer's first 30 days of gross profit pays for acquiring the next customer (*$100M Money Models*, launched Aug 16, 2025). The *$100M Lost Chapters* audiobook (Nov 2025, released on *The Game*) adds detail on CAC, attraction offers, and continuity.

**1. Value equation (Offers).** Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood) ÷ (Time delay × Effort and sacrifice). The levers are to raise the top two and push the bottom two toward zero. Cutting delay and effort usually beats promising a bigger outcome. Competitors compete on outcome claims; few compete on "fast and easy."

**2. Grand Slam Offer (Offers).**
1. Name the dream outcome.
2. List every obstacle the buyer will hit: before, during and after, and for each step of the process.
3. Turn each obstacle into a solution, stated as "how to ___ without ___".
4. Choose delivery vehicles for each solution: 1:1, group, or done-for-you; live or recorded; text, call, or in-person.
5. Trim and stack. Cut what's high-cost and low-value, then keep what's high-value and low-cost, plus a few premium items.

Then add the enhancers:
- **Scarcity** (limited quantity: seats, bonuses, slots).
- **Urgency** (limited time: cohorts, seasonal windows, price or bonus deadlines, exploding offers).
- **Bonuses.** Each one kills a specific objection, gets its own name and stated value, and is ideally worth more than the core.
- **Guarantees.** Four types: *unconditional*; *conditional* (refund or extra service if the buyer does X); *anti-guarantee* ("all sales final", with a reason given); and *implied/performance* (pay on results). Variants include service, credit-based, and outsized-refund guarantees, and guarantees can be stacked. Name every guarantee.

**3. Pricing (Offers + 2025–26 episodes).** Don't compete on price. Niche down and charge more. A higher price raises buyer commitment and funds better delivery. Show the price plainly. His Dec 2025 "If I started a business in 2026" advice: sell something very expensive to a few or something very cheap to many, because "the middle is where people die". Put a premium tier on everything, and sell capped 1:1 time early for cash and learning.

**4. MAGIC naming.** Build the offer name from five parts:
- **M**agnetic reason why (the reason for the offer)
- **A**vatar (who it's for)
- **G**oal (the result they want)
- **I**nterval (the time frame)
- **C**ontainer word (challenge, program, reset, check, blueprint)

**5. Core Four (Leads).**
- **Warm outreach:** people who know you.
- **Content:** posting free value.
- **Cold outreach:** people who don't know you, including in-person walk-ins.
- **Paid ads.**

With no budget and no audience, start with warm outreach. It's free, it's fastest, and it doubles as sales practice.

**6. Lead magnets (Leads).** A complete solution to a narrow problem that reveals the next problem your paid offer solves. There are three types: (i) reveal a problem (an assessment or diagnostic), (ii) a sample or trial, and (iii) one step of a multi-step process. Each can be delivered as software, information, a service, or a physical item. Name it by outcome, make it easy to consume, and give one clear call to action.

**7. Rule of 100.** Do 100 primary actions a day for 100 days: 100 outreach attempts, 100 minutes of content, or $100 of ads. Volume first, then quality.

**8. Warm outreach (Leads).**
- List everyone you know. Pick the channel that fits each relationship.
- Personalize the opener with **ACA**: *Acknowledge* what's going on with them, *Compliment* it, then *Ask* a question.
- Use the **"Do you know anyone who…?"** script. It turns a pitch into a request for help: interested people self-identify, and everyone else refers.
- Take the **first five free** (or near-free) in exchange for feedback and testimonials. Then raise the price in steps.
- Follow up with non-responders about 1–2 weeks later; stop after 2–3 attempts. Revive dormant leads with short "still looking for ___?" notes (Dean Jackson's 9-word email).

**9. Content (Leads).** Every piece needs a **Hook** (stops the scroll), **Retain** (lists, steps, stories) and **Reward** (the promised value, delivered). Give away the *what* and *why*, and sell the doing. Mostly give, sometimes ask. Repurpose one recording into many clips (the "content accordion", Ep 845).

**10. Lead getters (Leads).** There are four:
- **Customers** (referrals; a two-sided incentive sized to your CAC)
- **Employees**
- **Agencies**
- **Affiliates/partners** (paid per lead or per sale)

The order of leverage runs from you doing the Core Four, to others doing it for you, to lead getters who recruit more lead getters.

**11. More → Better → New.** Do more of what works. Then do it better (test hooks and first lines). Add a *new* channel only after the current one is maxed.

**12. Money Models (2025).** The sequence is attraction → upsell/downsell → continuity. Build one stage at a time.
- **Attraction offers:** win your money back; giveaways; decoy (a free or cheap version beside a premium one); buy X get Y; pay less now or pay more later.
- **Upsells:** classic ("you can't have X without Y"); menu (unsell, prescribe, A/B choice, card on file); anchor (show the 5–10× option first); rollover (credit what they paid toward the bigger offer).
- **Downsells:** payment plan (same total, split up); trial with penalty; feature downsell (less for less).
- **Continuity:** continuity bonus; continuity discount; waived fee.

The metric is 30-day gross profit per customer versus CAC. The Gym Launch model is the template:
- a free or cheap challenge;
- a supplement upsell within days;
- the challenge fee rolled over into an annual membership mid-challenge;
- later, a supplement line sold to the existing base.

**13. Selling (CLOSER).**
- **Clarify** why they're here.
- **Label** the problem.
- **Overview** past attempts and pain.
- **Sell the vacation**: the outcome, not the plane ride.
- **Explain away concerns.**
- **Reinforce** the decision.

**14. Proof before anything.** In his first gyms he needed before/after results and testimonials before marketing worked. He gave early clients a great deal in exchange for proof.

**The one-sentence takeaway for My4MLife.** Our problem in October was reach and proof, not the offer itself. Fifty-eight contacts, no testimonials, and a "middle"-priced product sold as the front door. Hormozi's fix: high-volume warm outreach now; proof from founding clients; a free or cheap front door (Brain Check, free GLP-1 call, $2.99 book); Push Patch moved to *upsell*; and Captain's Table as the premium "expensive to a few" engine, with a real downsell under it.

## Compliance lens: what does not transfer to regulated healthcare (read before (b))

Hormozi's examples come from gyms, agencies, and info products. A physician-reviewed Rx business is different. These are the guardrails every proposal below follows.

| Hormozi tactic | Why it breaks in healthcare | What we do instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Outcome guarantees** (Gym Launch: win your money back if you hit a weight-loss goal) | Texas Medical Board rule 22 TAC §164.3 treats ads that create unjustified expectations about results as misleading. Medicine can't guarantee outcomes, and physicians widely see refunds tied to clinical results as an ethics problem (KevinMD; American Med Spa Assn ethics column). | Guarantee **process, service and satisfaction**: "cleared or refunded", "your plan within 2 business days or the next month is on us", "the patch sticks or we replace it", "I come back at my cost". Never "lose X lb or your money back" on an Rx. |
| **Win-your-money-back** as an attraction offer | Same issue when the condition is a body outcome. | Allowed only for a **non-Rx** challenge where the condition is **behavior** (logging, attendance), not results. |
| **Testimonials and before/afters** | FTC Endorsement Guides (revised 2009, updated 2023): "results may vary" does **not** cure a testimonial that implies typical results. You need typical-results evidence, or testimonials about the *experience* (no needle, easy, fast service). The FTC Consumer Reviews & Testimonials Rule (effective Oct 21, 2024) bans fake, AI-generated, bought, or undisclosed insider reviews, with civil penalties (about $51,744 per violation at launch). The TMB treats false or misleading testimonials as deceptive advertising. | Written consent. Disclose any material connection (for example, "received her set at cost"). Prefer experience testimonials. Results photos are real, unretouched, labeled, and paired with what's typical. |
| **Health claims in hooks** | The FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec 2022) requires competent and reliable scientific evidence, generally human RCTs. A testimonial can't carry a claim we couldn't substantiate ourselves. | Keep the "may help support…" wording already on `/go/push-patch`. Manufacturer data stays labeled as manufacturer data. |
| **Stated bonus "values"** | An inflated "$2,000 value" is a deceptive price comparison. | State a value only if it's a real price we charge elsewhere (for example, the $12.99 CYS paperback or the $249 visit). Otherwise name the bonus without a dollar figure. |
| **Money-back language** | 16 CFR 239: say "money-back"/"satisfaction guarantee" only if you refund the full price on request; disclose conditions clearly; honor them promptly. | Every guarantee has its conditions printed next to it. |
| **Fake or soft scarcity** | It's a deception risk, and Hormozi himself teaches real limits. | Use only scarcity that is true: 2 Captain's Table starts a month, 4 seats, TJ's calendar days, real code end dates. |
| **"First five free"** on an Rx | A free prescription product invites inducement and fee questions, and the cost is real. | "Free" goes on *non-Rx* things: the visit or call, Brain Check, books, app, Zoom. Founding customers get **bonuses** (time with TJ, recognition), not free drugs. Glow testers at cost stay as already planned, with disclosure. |
| **Paying referrers** | Texas patient-solicitation and fee-splitting rules; channel-map rule #1 for Captain's Table. | No new cash incentives beyond the already-designed partner commission, and the blitz plan holds its first payout until the attorney OK. Captain's Table introducers get value, not fees. |
| **Giveaways** | You can't raffle a prescription. Sweepstakes need "no purchase necessary". | Give away non-Rx items only (books, a Captain's Day home reset without medical parts). |
| **Volume texting (Rule of 100)** | TCPA risk if texts are automated or go to people who never consented. | 1:1 texts typed and sent by TJ from his own phone to people who know him. Honor "stop". No PHI. Don't use old patient records for marketing. |
| **Paid ads** | Meta and Google require LegitScript for Rx telehealth. | Organic and outreach only this month (already in the plan). |
| **Discounts** | House rule: no discounts except Push Patch promo codes. Hormozi agrees: add value, don't cut price. | Every lever below uses bonuses, guarantees, payment terms, or credit rollovers instead of price cuts. |

**Fair criticisms of Hormozi to keep in view.**
- **Survivorship bias.** His lessons are drawn from what worked, with teams and ad budgets behind it.
- **Anecdotal case numbers.** The figures vary between retellings.
- **High-pressure closing** doesn't fit physician-trust selling.
- **Content volume** can burn out a solo founder. His own team posts hundreds of times a week.
- **"One offer, one avatar, one channel" vs. our plan.** Our blitz runs 6+ offers and 6+ channels at once. Hormozi would cut.

The sections below apply that discipline. One front-door motion (1:1 outreach). Two money offers that get most of the hours (Captain's Table for the few; GLP-1 plus Push Patch as the upsell for the many). Everything else rides along.

## (c) Rule-of-100 daily system (TJ's 10:00–4:00 window, Saturdays included)

**This replaces the blitz plan's 7:00–5:00 day with your stated availability.** It means fewer walk-ins per day (4 instead of 6–8) and the camera block moved to midday.

### What counts (100 "touches" a day)

| Action | Touches |
|---|---|
| A personal text, DM or email, typed by you to one person (a template edited for them is fine) | 1 |
| A phone dial (connect or voicemail) | 1 |
| A comment reply that starts a DM conversation | 1 |
| A walk-in at a spa or IV bar (counter card + one-pager left) | 5 |
| A live 1:1 meeting or coffee | 5 |

Not counted: posts, likes, scrolling, or group texts.

### The day

| Time | Block | Touches |

### Scripts (Claude personalizes each; you send from your own phone)

**1. Reconnect (ACA). Send this first; no pitch.**
> Hey Mike, it's TJ. Saw the pictures from Emily's wedding. She looked so happy, and you clean up well. How are you and Linda doing?

**2. After they reply: transition + "Do you know anyone?" (pick the variant from your notes).**
- **GLP-1:**
  > So good to hear. Quick one: I'm helping folks in midlife lose the belly without losing muscle or sharpness. It's physician-reviewed, a GLP-1 if your physician prescribes it, plus my eating method, and it starts with a free 10-minute call. Do you know anyone who's been talking about trying Ozempic or the new pill?
- **Push Patch:**
  > …I've got a once-a-week NAD+ and peptide patch, no needle, physician-reviewed. Do you know anyone who does IV drips or NAD shots and would rather skip the needle?
- **Captain's Table** (affluent contacts; aim for a call, never pitch by text):
  > …Do you know anyone who watched a parent go through dementia and has decided not to repeat it? I take two people a month into a program where I come to their home. I'd be glad to have coffee with them.
- **Partner:**
  > …Do you know anyone who owns a med spa, IV bar or facial studio? I've got a program that pays them per client order with zero inventory.

**3. Branches.**
- **"That's me":**
  > Love it. Want me to text you the link, or is a 5-minute call easier?

  Then send one link, tagged `?src=warm-<initials>`.
- **"My sister…":**
  > Thank you! Would you be comfortable sending a quick group text introducing us? I'll take it from there.
- **Neither:**
  > No worries at all. If anyone comes to mind, send them my way. Great catching up.

**4. Follow-up, day 3, no reply (touch 2 of 3 max):**
> Hey Mike, floating this back up. No rush at all.

**5. Dormant hand-raisers (the 9-word style):**
- > Are you still hoping to lose the belly before Christmas?
- > Still want to try the patch before the holidays?

**6. LinkedIn DM to a CEO or owner (ACA):** acknowledge their recent post, compliment it specifically, and ask one question about it. Bring up the Uninsured Decade talk or a coffee only after they reply.

**7. Phone call, 10 minutes (CLOSER):**
1. **Clarify:** "What made you reply?"
2. **Label:** "So it's the belly, and the 3 p.m. crash."
3. **Overview:** "What have you tried? How did it go?"
4. **Sell the vacation:** "Picture New Year's: jeans fit, afternoons are clear."
5. **Explain concerns.** Hormozi's three objection buckets are circumstances (time and money: pay-in-4, free call), other people (the spouse: "bring them on the call"), and self ("will it work for me?": process guarantees and physician review).
6. **Reinforce:** a welcome text within the hour and the next step booked.

**Copy rules on every script:**
- "Dr. TJ" only.
- "If your physician prescribes it."
- No outcome promises.
- No prices in public posts (DMs are fine).
- No PHI by text.
- No Amazon links in texts or emails.

## (d) Content plan (30–45 min a day, inside 12:45–1:30)

**Hormozi's rules, scaled to one person:**
- Every piece has a Hook (the first 3 seconds), Retain (a list, steps or a story), and Reward (one usable takeaway).
- Give away the *what* and *why*; the *how*, done for you, is the offer.
- Volume beats polish.
- Repurpose one recording into many clips.
- Double down on the top two hooks weekly.

**Daily (30 min record + 15 min engage):**
- Record **3 vertical takes of 30–60 seconds**, using the content pack's 30 hooks and 10 scripts (`docs/launch/mass-market/day1-content-pack-2026-10-31.md`).
- Claude writes captions and UTMs; you post the same file natively to **Facebook Reels, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts**, plus LinkedIn for the money, estate and CEO angle. TikTok is lowest priority for this audience.
- Spend 15 minutes replying to comments. A reply that opens a DM counts toward the Rule of 100.

**Weekly (the "accordion"):** record the free weekly Zoom (your solo teaching segment only; no attendee faces without consent). Claude marks 3–5 timestamps worth clipping, and you or a free clipping tool cut them.

**Hook toolbox** (Hormozi's headline elements, applied):

| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| Recency | "November is Alzheimer's Awareness Month. Here's what to watch for at the Thanksgiving table." |
| Relevance | "If you're over 40 and the belly won't move…" |
| Proximity | "Fort Worth, this one's for you." |
| Conflict / myth | "10,000 steps was a 1960s pedometer slogan." |
| Unusual | "I stopped counting calories and started counting to 32." |
| Ongoing series | "Day 9 of my own patch." / "Week 2 of the Holiday Hold." |
| Sex-specific | "(Men) ED is often the first warning light." / "(Women) Menopause brain fog is real." |

**Retain formats:** "3 mistakes…", "the 4 counts", a 30-second patient-style story (composite or consented), and answering one comment on camera.

**Reward + one CTA:**
- One takeaway per video.
- The only CTA in public posts is the **free Thanksgiving Brain Check** (or the free GLP-1 call on belly content).
- No prices or Rx product pitches in organic posts.

**Mix:** about 4 of every 5 posts pure value, 1 an invitation (the Nov 29 Zoom, a founding-tester story with disclosure).

**Measure weekly:** 3-second views ÷ impressions (hook), average watch % (retain), profile visits and Brain Check starts by `utm_content` (reward). Kill the bottom half of the hooks and re-shoot the top two in new words.

**Expectation (honest):** content is the slowest of the Core Four. In 20 days it builds proof and recognition for the warm outreach ("I saw your video") more than it makes direct sales. That's why it gets 45 minutes, not 4 hours.
