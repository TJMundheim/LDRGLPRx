## 1. Jay Abraham: host-beneficiary, joint ventures, risk reversal, preeminence

**What he actually did.** Abraham's best-known move is the *host-beneficiary* deal. You find a business that already has the trust of the customers you want (the host). The host introduces your offer to its own list, in its own voice, as an endorsement. In return the host gets a cut of revenue, or simply looks good by giving its customers something valuable. The classic version is an *endorsed mailing*: a letter on the host's letterhead, signed by the host, saying "I found this and I want you to have it." Your offer and response device are enclosed. Abraham's other two pillars are:

- **Risk reversal:** the seller, not the buyer, carries the risk of the purchase. Strong guarantees and free trials, stated up front, are how he frames it.
- **The Strategy of Preeminence:** you act as the client's trusted advisor and put their outcome ahead of your sale. You advise them even when the advice is "don't buy from me."

**Why it worked.** An endorsement transfers years of trust in one letter. The Robbins-hosted Abraham material makes the point that an endorsement "cuts through" the trust-building steps, and that performance-based deals cost the beneficiary nothing up front. The host has no acquisition cost because it already owns the relationship. Risk reversal removes the buyer's last objection, and preeminence makes the seller someone the buyer wants to refer.

**How TJ applies it this month.**
- **The spa partner program is already a host-beneficiary deal, but it is being run as a counter-card deal.** The Abraham version is stronger: each signed partner sends **one endorsed email or text to its own client list**. Claude writes it; the partner reviews it and sends it under its own name: "We've started carrying something for the days between your drips…" with the partner's `?ref=` link. A counter card reaches whoever walks in this month. An endorsed email reaches the spa's whole list in a day. **Make "you'll send one email to your list" part of the partner yes.**
- **Estate attorneys, advisors and the Lead Concepts hosts (Marla's introductions, Chris Wear's seminar hosts):** the host invites its clients to a private educational briefing (*The Uninsured Decade*), framed as a client-appreciation event. Per the channel map's rule, **no referral fees to licensed professionals.** The host's payoff is value instead: a free talk for its clients, a book on every seat, and looking good in front of them. That is still host-beneficiary; Abraham's hosts were often paid in goodwill.
- **Risk reversal on the Push Patch:** state it plainly at checkout and on the partner card: *"If the physician doesn't approve you, you're refunded in full."* That is already true. Consider adding a first-order guarantee (for example, a refund on an unopened set, or a reorder credit if they don't notice a difference). That is TJ's call, but at the ~$210–$260 margin it is affordable.
- **Preeminence on every Captain's Table call:** lead with "what's the right answer for you?" and be willing to say "you don't need the Captain's Table; start with the Brain Check and the book." At $50K, a buyer who trusts that you would turn them away is the buyer who signs.

## 2. Russell Brunson: Perfect Webinar, Dream 100, value ladder

**What he actually did.**
- **Perfect Webinar** (*Expert Secrets*): sell **one big idea** (the "One Thing"). Teach **three secrets**, each one breaking a false belief the audience holds about the vehicle, about themselves, or about outside obstacles. Then **"the stack"**: list every component of the offer with its value, reveal the price, add a guarantee and real urgency, and close. Summaries say about 60–70% of the time is teaching.
- **Dream 100** (*Traffic Secrets*, adapted from Chet Holmes): list the people who already hold your dream customers' attention (podcasters, newsletter owners, community leaders), build relationships with them, and get promoted to their audiences. "Work your way in" (relationships) before you "buy your way in" (ads). The other two ideas from the book are **Hook → Story → Offer** for every piece of content, and **build your own list**.
- **Value ladder** (*DotCom Secrets*): free → low ticket → core → high ticket, each rung qualifying buyers for the next.

**Why it worked.** The webinar moves a cold-to-warm audience through belief shifts in one sitting, and live urgency is real. Brunson's team says the same script works live, automated and on stage. Vendors claim live converts better than automated, but nobody publishes clean data on that.

**Can a live Zoom sell high ticket?** In Brunson's own model, webinars sell **direct-checkout offers up to the low thousands**. Above that, the webinar sells **an application and a call**, and the call closes. For TJ:
- **Push Patch ($550–$795): yes**, sell it directly on a live Zoom, with a deadline.
- **Captain's Table ($50K): no.** The Zoom ends with "four seats a year, by application," and every applicant gets a call within 24 hours.

**How TJ applies it this month.**
- **Move the live session inside the window.** The planned "Thanksgiving Brain Check" Zoom is on Sun Nov 29, nine days after the window closes. Hold a **live pre-Thanksgiving session on Tue Nov 17 or Thu Nov 19** instead, called something like *"What to notice at the Thanksgiving table (and what to do about it)."* Keep the Nov 29 session as a follow-up.
  - **One Thing:** "Alzheimer's is a disease of middle age that shows up when we're old, so the window to act is now."
  - **Three secrets:** (1) the early signs aren't memory; (2) the insulting behaviors you can stop this week; (3) what repair looks like.
  - **Stack:** the Push Patch plus the Logbook plus the app plus the weekly Zoom, with a deadline of Nov 30 (the Thanksgiving code). Then one line on the Captain's Table application.
- **Dream 100 → "Dream 15" for 20 days.** A real Dream 100 takes months. Pick the 15 people who already hold the audiences TJ needs: Randall Beasley, Victor Calabrese, Chris Wear, Marla, Karla Nunn and Liffort Hobley (NFL alumni), 3 C12/Vistage chairs, 3 church/business-breakfast hosts, and the 3 best spa owners. Each one gets a specific, generous offer: a free talk for their people.
- **The value ladder exists already** (Brain Check → books → Push Patch/GLP-1 → Captain's Table). Don't build new rungs this month.

## 3. Dan Kennedy, Gary Vaynerchuk, Codie Sanchez

### Dan Kennedy: direct response for professionals, lumpy mail, the affluent
**What he did.** Kennedy taught professionals (doctors, chiropractors, dentists, advisors) to market like direct-response businesses:
- every piece asks for a specific, measurable response with a deadline;
- the list matters more than the creative;
- follow-up sequences beat one-off pieces.

**"Lumpy mail" / the shock-and-awe package:** a dimensional mailing (an object in the envelope, or a box of books, letters and proof) opens first and stands out. Practitioners warn it **loses money on broad lists** and only works on a small, high-value list. In *No B.S. Marketing to the Affluent*, Kennedy argues that affluent buyers need **"points of affinity"** (shared identity and experience) before they buy. He also recommends **membership language** and **deliberate, engineered referrals** instead of hoping for word of mouth.

**Why it worked.** Professionals have high-value transactions, so a $30 package aimed at someone worth $50K is cheap. Affluent buyers are rarely reached by ads; they are reached through relationships and private settings.

**How TJ applies it.**
- **A shock-and-awe box to the top 10–12 Captain's Table prospects.** Include:
  - a signed *Begin with the End in Mind*;
  - the *Uninsured Decade* calculator card with their own numbers, if known;
  - a handwritten note;
  - a printed one-page "what the 30 days look like";
  - a deadline: "two December starts remain, applications close Nov 18."
  About $25–$40 each. Hand-deliver locally where possible.
- **Affinity:** every Captain's Table conversation opens on shared ground: faith, football, ranch, being 59 and building a legacy, a parent with dementia.
- **Engineered referrals:** after every good call, ask "who are the two people you'd want at the table with you?" Ask the same of every satisfied Push Patch buyer.

### Gary Vaynerchuk: document don't create, $1.80, local
**What he did.**
- **"Document, don't create":** record your real work and day instead of producing polished content.
- **"$1.80":** leave your "two cents" on about 90 relevant posts a day, so you show up where conversations already happen.
- **Local businesses:** cross-promote with neighbors, and once content performs organically, put about $100 a week behind it within roughly 10 miles.

**Why it works.** It is free, and it compounds attention. But it compounds over months.

**How TJ applies it.**
- The 30–60 second daily video is already in the plan. Make it documentary, not scripted: film the walk-ins, the partner wins, the ranch prep, and his own patch.
- Do a **local $1.80** every day for 20 minutes: comment usefully in Fort Worth/Aledo/Benbrook Facebook groups, on the spa partners' posts, and on local reporters' posts.
- Most importantly, **get partners to repost his videos**. That is host-beneficiary at the content level.
- Paid boosting of Rx products is restricted (see §4), so don't boost them this month.

Her work is on *buying* "boring" Main Street businesses (raise prices, add technology), not on customer acquisition. The search found nothing useful on local B2B referral deals, so it is not applied here.

**Compliance limits that shape tactics (factual, for planning):**
- **Ads:** Google requires certification (LegitScript in the US) to advertise prescription-drug services, and even certified advertisers can't promote the drugs themselves in ads, landing pages or keywords. Meta requires LegitScript for telehealth and bars Rx sales in ads. **Paid ads for the Push Patch or GLP-1 are not available this month**, which the full-court press plan already notes.
- **Testimonials:** under the FTC's 2023 Endorsement Guides, a testimonial that implies a result must reflect what people *generally* achieve, or the ad must state the typical result. "Results vary" alone doesn't protect you. Reposted reviews become your endorsements. The FTC's Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec 2022) says testimonials can't stand in for substantiation, and it generally expects RCT-level evidence for health claims. The FTC also finalized a Consumer Reviews and Testimonials rule in 2024 that bans fake reviews and testimonials.
- **What follows for the glow testers:** use real, consented before/afters with the typical-result framing. Never use edited or AI images.

## 5. Med spa, IV bar and longevity clinic marketing that works

**What they actually do.**
- **Referrals** are described as the cheapest, best channel. Rewards run about $50–$100 credit per referral. The common failure is tracking, which our Stripe codes solve.
- **Memberships** are pushed hard; the "2–4× spend" claim comes from dental/optometry data *(vendor)*.
- **Events** convert: open houses, product demos, "lunch and learns", and pop-ups. Vendors note some people must *experience* a treatment before they commit.
- **Local influencers:** they have to be genuinely local and able to visit.
- **Seasonality:** Q4 is the strongest quarter. Gift card and holiday bundles peak in November–December, and spas routinely run "buy a $200 gift card, get $50" promotions from Black Friday through Christmas *(vendor figures unverified)*.

**Why it works.** Spa clients already buy on trust, in person, from staff they like. Events give staff a reason to call their list and give clients a reason to buy that night.

**How TJ applies it (so partners actually sell).**
- **Depth over breadth.** 25 signed partners who don't sell are worth $0. The med-spa pattern says what moves product is **staff motivation plus an event plus the list.** Sign 12–15, and **activate 5**.
- **"Patch Night" at the best 3 partners (Weeks 2–3).** The spa invites its list (endorsed email, §1). TJ gives a 15-minute talk ("the days between your drips" / "the glow from inside"). Glow-tester results are shown where consented. Orders are placed on the spot on a tablet with the partner's code.
- **Staff spiff:** a small bonus from our share to the staff member who makes the first sale (for example, $25). It aligns the person behind the counter, not only the owner.
- **Ride the holiday season** without new discounts: the patch as a **holiday gift** ("a gift for the person who has everything"), sold through the partner's existing holiday gift-card push. The partner's own code is the only discount.

## 6. High-ticket concierge and longevity sales ($15K–$50K+)

**What they actually do.**
- **Biograph** (Attia co-founded) charges about $7,500 (Core) to about $15,000/yr (Black).
- **Fountain Life** sells tiered memberships into founder communities (A360, Robbins).
- **Abundance360** itself ($12.5K–$55K tiers) is **application-only, waitlisted, and sells out in advance.**

The pattern across these:
1. **Application, not checkout.** It qualifies the buyer and signals scarcity. Coaching vendors claim applications lift close rates 40–60% *(vendor)*.
2. **Selling happens in a conversation, not on a page.** Operators report 18–70% close rates on $30–50K offers from qualified calls *(anecdotal)*.
3. **Small in-person events** are the fastest way to sell $10K+ when time is short *(vendor)*.
4. **Referrals inside trusted communities** do most of the work.

The financial-advisor dinner-seminar industry (directly relevant to Lead Concepts) publishes the most data:
- meal-seminar mail response is about 0.4–1.6%;
- no-shows run 15–20% from mail and up to 60% from social ads;
- the best advisors **book the appointment face to face before the guest leaves** *(trade press, mixed vintage)*.

**Why it works.** A $50K buyer is buying *you*, and trust at that level is built in person, in a room of peers, with someone they respect vouching for you.

**How TJ applies it.**
- **The Nov 14 ranch dinner is the correct play.** Three refinements:
  1. **Invitation through the host.** Randall and Victor invite by name; the hosts' credibility is the trust transfer.
  2. **Book the follow-up at the table:** "I'd like to come see you Tuesday. Does 10 or 2 work?" Do this *before* guests leave, the advisor-seminar rule, instead of waiting for applications on Monday.
  3. **Real scarcity, stated once:** four seats a year, two December starts.
- **Run a second, smaller close setting if the first slips:** a breakfast or a lunch of 4–6 at a private club or at a host's home in Week 3. Don't stake both $50K seats on one evening.
- **Every Captain's Table conversation ends in a calendar event, never a "think about it."**

## 7. Local media and podcast booking for doctors and authors

**What works.**
- **Local TV producers** want a local expert who speaks plain English on camera, a **visual**, and a **ready-made segment**: write the segment for them, often as "5 tips". Pitch with a headline and a hook in the first two sentences, and give lead time.
- **Newsjacking a recurring calendar moment** is the most reliable hook:
  - **November is National Alzheimer's Disease Awareness Month and National Family Caregivers Month.**
  - Every year the Alzheimer's Association pushes a **"warning signs to watch for at holiday gatherings"** story, and reports Helpline call spikes around Thanksgiving. Local outlets reprint it year after year. TJ's "what to notice at the Thanksgiving table" fits the story producers already run.
- **Expert-source platforms after HARO** (HARO and Connectively shut in Dec 2024):
  - **Source of Sources** (free, from HARO's founder Peter Shankman);
  - **HARO relaunched by Featured** (2025);
  - **Featured.com**, **Qwoted** (free tiers; paid ~$49–$99/mo);
  - **Help a B2B Writer / MentionMatch.**
- **Podcasts:** write your own pitch. One host rejects about 95% of agency pitches because they are generic. Listen to 2–3 episodes, and offer specific episode titles. Booking guides say to target 100–200 shows over time.

**Why it works.** Producers and hosts need a guest who is easy to book. A local expert tied to a story they already run is a low-risk yes.

**How TJ applies it (and its limits).**
- Pitch the five local outlets with a **finished segment**: *"5 things to notice at the Thanksgiving table — and the 3 that aren't normal aging,"* from a Fort Worth doctor and author. Offer a local family who will speak on camera if one exists. **The pitch window closes by about Nov 9–12 for Thanksgiving week.**
- The CTA from any media hit is the **free Brain Check**, which feeds the list.
- **Honest limit:** media and podcasts produce contacts and credibility; they rarely produce paid orders inside 20 days. Most podcasts *release* weeks after recording. Cap media at about 6 hours total and count it as January's pipeline, not November revenue.

## 8. Authors who turned books into businesses

**What they actually did.**
- **Brunson's "free + shipping"** book funnel: the book is free, the buyer pays about $7.95–$9.95 shipping, and is immediately offered upsells (one buyer reports ending at about $99). The book funds acquisition and qualifies buyers.
- **Scribe's model:** book sales alone rarely pay. The money is in clients and speaking, and Scribe's own examples credit the book with landing enterprise clients *(vendor)*.
- **Kennedy and the professional-practice tradition:** the book is the "business card" you hand to a prospect, and the lead-in for the shock-and-awe package.
- **Gifting copies directly** to the right 25–100 people is the most common route to contracts and speaking *(vendor anecdotes)*.

**Why it works.** A book is cheap proof of expertise that the prospect keeps on the desk.

**How TJ applies it.**
- **Don't build a free-plus-shipping funnel this month.** It needs traffic to feed it, and TJ doesn't have that yet. Build it in January.
- **Use the book as the ticket and the gift.** Every Captain's Table prospect, every partner owner, every host and every producer gets a signed physical copy. Every talk ends with "the book is on your chair; the Brain Check QR is on page one."
- **Kindle at $2.99** builds the Amazon author page; it is not revenue this month.

## Traps to avoid (things that waste a 20-day window)

1. **Building instead of selling.** New pages, app features, the Apple Developer/AirPods test, book formatting, or a free-plus-shipping funnel. If it doesn't put TJ in front of a buyer before Nov 20, it waits until December.
2. **Breadth over activation.** Signing 25 partners who never run an event or send an email. Chasing 1,000 contacts instead of 40 real conversations.
3. **Pitching the Captain's Table from a stage, on Zoom checkout, or at the dinner table.** At $50K it is application → conversation → calendar.
4. **Ending a high-ticket conversation with "think about it."** Book the next meeting before anyone leaves.
5. **Paid ads for Rx offers.** Google and Meta block prescription-drug promotion without certification, and drugs can't be promoted even with it. Money and accounts both get burned.
6. **Cold blasts and bought lists.** MedVi's affiliate email spam drew a class action. Never send to people who didn't opt in, and hold partners to the same rule in writing.
7. **Fake or edited proof.** AI-altered before/afters or stock "patients" (MedVi again). Testimonials must be real, consented and typical, and "results vary" alone doesn't cover an atypical result.
8. **Discount-led selling.** Stacking codes erodes the ~$210–$260 margin and breaks the standing rule (promo codes only on the Push Patch, partner codes as the default). Risk reversal and pay-in-4 do the same job without cutting price.
9. **Five offers in one conversation.** One door per audience: the affluent → the Captain's Table; spa clients → the Push Patch; belly → GLP-1; worried families → the Brain Check.
10. **Counting media and podcasts as November revenue.** Most podcasts release after the window. Cap prep at about an hour each.
11. **Scheduling the close after the window.** Thanksgiving week is dead. Every follow-up that matters lands by Thu Nov 19. Don't plan the first live Zoom for Nov 29.
12. **An automated or evergreen webinar.** With no traffic it sells nothing. Live only, this month.
