## Creatine for muscle and performance — overwhelming evidence

### Definitive consensus document — ISSN Position Stand

- **Citation:** Kreider RB, Kalman DS, Antonio J, et al. "International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: safety and efficacy of creatine supplementation in exercise, sport, and medicine." *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*. 2017;14:18. [PubMed 28615996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28615996/)
- **Authority:** The most-cited consensus document on creatine. 30 contributing authors. Updated periodically.
- **Key conclusions (verbatim):**
  - *"Creatine monohydrate is the most effective ergogenic nutritional supplement currently available to athletes."*
  - *"Creatine monohydrate is well tolerated, generally safe, and increases muscle mass, strength, and exercise performance during repeated, short-duration, high-intensity exercise."*
  - 3–5g/day chronic dosing is sufficient. Loading phase optional (accelerates saturation but not necessary).
  - Long-term safety: 30+ years of supplementation data, no significant adverse effects in healthy adults

### Sarcopenia mitigation — Chilibeck meta-analysis 2017

- **Citation:** Chilibeck PD, Kaviani M, Candow DG, Zello GA. "Effect of creatine supplementation during resistance training on lean tissue mass and muscular strength in older adults: a meta-analysis." *Open Access Journal of Sports Medicine*. 2017;8:213-226. [PubMed 29138605](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29138605/)
- **Design:** Meta-analysis of 22 RCTs in older adults (mean age ≥50)
- **Result:** Creatine + resistance training vs resistance training alone:
  - **Lean tissue mass: +1.4 kg additional gain** (95% CI, 1.0–1.8 kg)
  - **Chest press strength: +9.5 kg additional gain**
  - **Leg press strength: +14.0 kg additional gain**
- **Significance:** This is the single most-cited evidence base for the "creatine prevents sarcopenia" claim. Direct measurement, large sample pooled across 22 RCTs, statistically robust.

### Strength meta-analyses — Lanhers et al.

- **Lower limb strength:** Lanhers C, Pereira B, Naughton G, et al. "Creatine supplementation and lower limb strength performance: A systematic review and meta-analyses." *Sports Medicine*. 2015;45(9):1285-1294. [PubMed 26049792](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26049792/)
  - **Result:** Creatine significantly increased lower-limb strength performance (effect size 0.336, P<.001)
- **Upper limb strength:** Lanhers C, Pereira B, Naughton G, et al. "Creatine Supplementation and Upper Limb Strength Performance: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis." *Sports Medicine*. 2017;47(1):163-173. [PubMed 27328852](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27328852/)
  - **Result:** Creatine significantly increased upper-limb strength performance (effect size 0.265, P<.001), particularly for short-duration exercises (≤3 minutes)

### Body composition meta-analysis — Forbes et al. 2022

- **Citation:** Forbes SC, Candow DG, Smith-Ryan AE, et al. "Creatine monohydrate supplementation and lean tissue mass: A meta-analysis." *Nutrients*. 2022;14(15):3038. [PubMed 35893892](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35893892/)
- **Result:** Creatine + resistance training across studies showed consistent lean mass increases vs training alone. Effect was robust across age, training status, and protocol variations.

### Pre-creatine vs post-creatine performance — well-documented mechanism

- **Phosphocreatine system** — creatine acts as an ATP buffer during high-energy-demand bursts (sprinting, lifting, intense interval work)
- 5g/day chronic dosing increases intramuscular phosphocreatine stores by **~20%**, providing measurable performance headroom during repeated bouts of maximal exercise
- This mechanism is the most-replicated finding in sports nutrition literature

## L-citrulline for exercise performance — strong evidence

### Citrulline malate and resistance training — Pérez-Guisado & Jakeman 2010

- **Citation:** Pérez-Guisado J, Jakeman PM. "Citrulline malate enhances athletic anaerobic performance and relieves muscle soreness." *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*. 2010;24(5):1215-1222. [PubMed 20386132](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20386132/)
- **Design:** Double-blind, two-period crossover. 41 men. 8g citrulline malate 1 hour before workout
- **Results:**
  - **52.92% increase in repetitions performed** during pectoral training (vs placebo)
  - **Significant reduction in muscle soreness** at 24 and 48 hours post-exercise (40% reduction at 24h, 41% reduction at 48h)
- **Significance:** Direct demonstration that L-citrulline acutely improves training capacity and accelerates recovery

### Citrulline meta-analysis — Trexler et al. 2019

- **Citation:** Gonzalez AM, Trexler ET. "Effects of Citrulline Supplementation on Exercise Performance in Humans: A Review of the Current Literature." *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*. 2020;34(5):1480-1495. [PubMed 31977835](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31977835/)
- **Result:** Comprehensive review concluded citrulline supplementation improves exercise performance, particularly in:
  - Resistance training (more reps to failure)
  - High-intensity, short-duration exercise
  - Reduced perceived exertion
  - Reduced post-exercise muscle soreness

### Mechanism

L-citrulline bypasses hepatic first-pass metabolism that limits L-arginine bioavailability. Increased arginine → NO production → vasodilation → improved oxygen and nutrient delivery to working muscle → improved performance + reduced fatigue accumulation. The malate component contributes to TCA cycle intermediates that support aerobic ATP production.

## Beetroot / dietary nitrate for exercise performance — strong evidence

### Comprehensive meta-analysis — Domínguez et al. 2017

- **Citation:** Domínguez R, Cuenca E, Maté-Muñoz JL, et al. "Effects of Beetroot Juice Supplementation on Cardiorespiratory Endurance in Athletes. A Systematic Review." *Nutrients*. 2017;9(1):43. [PubMed 28067808](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28067808/)
- **Result:** Systematic review of 23 RCTs. Beetroot juice supplementation:
  - **Reduced oxygen cost of submaximal exercise** (improved exercise economy)
  - **Increased time to exhaustion** during endurance exercise
  - **Modest performance improvements** in cycling time trials (1–3%)
- **Mechanism:** Dietary nitrate → NO → improved mitochondrial efficiency (lower O₂ cost per unit ATP produced) + improved muscle perfusion

### Resistance training context — Domínguez et al. 2018

- Subsequent work showed beetroot nitrate also benefits high-intensity, resistance-style exercise — not just endurance. Effects on repetition volume and time-to-fatigue.

### Why beetroot pairs with citrulline

Two parallel NO-production pathways: citrulline → arginine → NO (eNOS-dependent) AND dietary nitrate → nitrite → NO (independent of eNOS, particularly active in hypoxic conditions like working muscle). The two pathways are additive, not redundant.

## Taurine for performance and recovery — moderate evidence

### Taurine and exercise

- **Citation:** Waldron M, Patterson SD, Tallent J, Jeffries O. "The Effects of an Oral Taurine Dose and Supplementation Period on Endurance Exercise Performance in Humans: A Meta-Analysis." *Sports Medicine*. 2018;48(5):1247-1253. [PubMed 29546641](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29546641/)
- **Result:** Meta-analysis showed acute taurine supplementation (1–6g) before exercise improved endurance performance metrics. Effect size small but consistent.
- **Mechanism:** Taurine supports muscle contraction efficiency, calcium handling, antioxidant function during exercise stress

### Taurine for recovery

- Reduces muscle damage markers (CK, LDH) and oxidative stress markers post-exercise in multiple trials
