## Creatine for cognitive function — strong, replicated evidence

### Landmark RCT — Rae et al. 2003

- **Citation:** Rae C, Digney AL, McEwan SR, Bates TC. "Oral creatine monohydrate supplementation improves brain performance: a double-blind, placebo-controlled, cross-over trial." *Proceedings of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences*. 2003 Oct;270(1529):2147-50. [PubMed 14561278](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14561278/)
- **Design:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover. 45 vegetarian adults
- **Intervention:** 5g/day creatine monohydrate for 6 weeks
- **Primary results:**
  - **Significant improvement in working memory** (backward digit span, P=.003)
  - **Significant improvement in intelligence test performance** (Raven's Advanced Progressive Matrices, P<.0001)
- **Why vegetarians?** Lower baseline brain creatine stores → larger measurable effect. Subsequent studies replicated in omnivores with smaller but real effect sizes.

**Author conclusion (verbatim from abstract):** *"Creatine supplementation had a significant positive effect (P<0.0001) on both working memory (backward digit span) and intelligence (Raven's Advanced Progressive Matrices), both tasks that require speed of processing."*

### Meta-analysis — Avgerinos et al. 2018

- **Citation:** Avgerinos KI, Spyrou N, Bougioukas KI, Kapogiannis D. "Effects of creatine supplementation on cognitive function of healthy individuals: A systematic review of randomized controlled trials." *Experimental Gerontology*. 2018 Jul 15;108:166-173. [PubMed 29704637](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29704637/)
- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of 6 RCTs
- **Result:** Creatine supplementation **improves short-term memory and intelligence/reasoning in healthy individuals**. Effect sizes were larger in:
  - Older adults
  - Vegetarians and vegans
  - Adults under physical or mental stress
- **Author conclusion:** Creatine supplementation has a significant positive effect on cognitive processing, particularly under conditions of metabolic stress or low baseline brain creatine.

### Acute high-dose creatine for sleep-deprived cognition — Gordji-Nejad et al. 2024

- **Citation:** Gordji-Nejad A, Matusch A, Kleedörfer S, et al. "Single dose creatine improves cognitive performance and induces changes in cerebral high energy phosphates during sleep deprivation." *Scientific Reports*. 2024;14(1):4937. [PubMed 38418482](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38418482/)
- **Design:** Sleep-deprivation crossover trial. Single 0.35 g/kg creatine dose (~25g for a 70kg adult)
- **Result:** **Cognitive performance improved within 3 hours** of ingestion. ¹H/³¹P MRS imaging showed measurable increases in cerebral high-energy phosphates (phosphocreatine + ATP). Sleep-deprived cognitive decline was meaningfully attenuated.
- **Significance:** First direct demonstration that creatine acutely affects brain energetics and cognition — not just chronic supplementation effect. Critical for the "demanding day" use case (high-stakes meeting under poor sleep).

### Brain phosphocreatine elevation — mechanistic confirmation

- **Citation:** Dechent P, Pouwels PJ, Wilken B, et al. "Increase of total creatine in human brain after oral supplementation of creatine-monohydrate." *Am J Physiol*. 1999;277(3):R698-R704. [PubMed 10484486](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10484486/)
- **Result:** Brain creatine concentration increases **5–15%** after 4 weeks of oral creatine supplementation, measured by ¹H magnetic resonance spectroscopy.
- **Significance:** Settles the question of whether oral creatine reaches the brain. Yes — measurably.

### Creatine for older-adult cognition — recent evidence

- **Citation:** Forbes SC, Cordingley DM, Cornish SM, et al. "Effects of Creatine Supplementation on Brain Function and Health." *Nutrients*. 2022;14(5):921. [PubMed 35267907](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35267907/)
- **Summary:** Comprehensive narrative review covering brain phosphocreatine, mitochondrial function, neuroinflammation. Concludes that creatine supplementation is a "promising" intervention for cognitive aging, with the strongest effects seen in:
  - Older adults (>50)
  - Those under sleep deprivation or stress
  - Vegetarians/vegans

## L-citrulline + beetroot for cerebral blood flow — moderate-to-strong evidence

### Beetroot juice and cerebral perfusion — Wightman et al. 2015

- **Citation:** Wightman EL, Haskell-Ramsay CF, Thompson KG, et al. "Dietary nitrate modulates cerebral blood flow parameters and cognitive performance in humans: A double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover investigation." *Physiology & Behavior*. 2015 Oct 1;149:149-58. [PubMed 26037632](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26037632/)
- **Design:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover. Acute beetroot juice (containing ~430mg nitrate)
- **Result:** Beetroot juice **increased cerebral blood flow during cognitive task performance** (measured by near-infrared spectroscopy) and **improved performance on demanding mental arithmetic** (Serial Subtractions task).
- **Mechanism:** Dietary nitrate → nitrite (via oral microbiome) → NO → cerebral vasodilation → improved perfusion during high-demand cognitive tasks.

### Cerebral blood flow as the cognitive longevity lever

- Cerebral blood flow declines ~0.4–0.5% per year in adulthood (Schultz et al., Stoquart-ElSankari et al.)
- Improving endothelial function and NO availability is one of the few directly-actionable levers for maintaining cerebral perfusion across decades
- L-citrulline + nitrate are the two most-evidenced OTC interventions for endothelial NO production

### Pycnogenol + cognition — optional premium variant

- **Citation:** Pipingas A, Silberstein RB, Vitetta L, et al. "Improved cognitive performance after dietary supplementation with a Pinus radiata bark extract formulation." *Phytother Res*. 2008;22(9):1168-74.
- Pycnogenol (French maritime pine bark, mechanistically similar) improved cognitive performance in healthy adults; subsequent studies confirm cerebral blood flow / endothelial mechanism

## Taurine for cognitive function — moderate evidence, supporting role

- **Citation:** Singh P, Gollapalli K, Mangiola S, et al. "Taurine deficiency as a driver of aging." *Science*. 2023;380(6649):eabn9257.
- High-impact 2023 paper showing taurine supplementation extends healthspan in multiple mammalian models. Specifically:
  - Improved mitochondrial function
  - Reduced cellular senescence
  - Improved cognitive outcomes in aged mice
- Human implication uncertain but mechanistically aligned with MitoVita's mitochondrial-and-vascular thesis

## What MitoVita does NOT have direct evidence for (in cognition)

- We cannot claim creatine "prevents Alzheimer's" — Alzheimer's prevention is a regulated medical claim
- We cannot claim "increases IQ by X points" without RCT-specific support and structure/function compliance
- We should not overclaim acute cognitive performance benefits for healthy, well-slept users — the strongest effects are in sleep-deprived, older, vegetarian, or otherwise metabolically-stressed individuals
