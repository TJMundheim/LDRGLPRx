# Temperature Environment Roadmap — TJ Review

**Drafted 2026-04-28. Internal product roadmap — NOT live on the website.**

Temperature is one of the most well-studied and clinically actionable levers in healthspan optimization. Controlled heat and cold exposure trigger adaptive stress responses — heat shock proteins, norepinephrine surges, mitochondrial biogenesis — that accumulate over months into measurable improvements in cardiovascular health, cognitive function, body composition, and mood. This doc covers three delivery vehicles: dry sauna, cold plunge / cryotherapy, and mineral hot bath; plus contrast therapy protocols, TJ's Texas DIY build guide, and Phase 1 launch priorities.

## 1. Sauna (Dry / Far-Infrared / Traditional Swedish)

**Positioning:** "Heat shock proteins, BDNF expression, cardiovascular adaptation, lipid-soluble toxin excretion. Sauna is one of the most studied interventions for healthspan — Finnish epidemiological data shows dramatic all-cause mortality reductions at 4–7x/week and 20+ minute sessions. For men 35–60, it is among the strongest longevity signals in the literature. Begin with the end in mind: the man who saunas four times a week at 60 looks and performs like someone a decade younger."

**Evidence quality:** Strong. Multiple large Finnish cohort studies (Laukkanen et al., 2018 JAMA Intern Med; ongoing Kuopio Ischemic Heart Disease cohort) showing dose-dependent reductions in cardiovascular events, dementia incidence, and all-cause mortality at 4–7 sessions/week.

### Education content

**Far-infrared vs traditional Swedish vs steam — quick decision tree:**
- Live in apartment or no outdoor space → FIR blanket or pop-up tent
- Have a spare room → 1- or 2-person FIR cabin
- Have a backyard + want the real Finnish experience → barrel sauna
- Already have a steam shower → add dry heat as complement; steam alone lacks the high-heat signal
- Want the full therapeutic dose → traditional Swedish at 175–195°F

**Recommended cadence:** 4x/week × 20 min minimum @ 175–195°F (traditional) or 130–160°F surface temp (FIR). The Finnish longevity data was built on this dose. Less is still beneficial; less than 2x/week loses most of the cardiovascular signal.

**Pre/post protocol:**
- Pre: 500ml water + electrolytes 30 min before
- Post: cold plunge or cold shower immediately after (contrast protocol) OR 10-min rest + 500ml water
- Avoid: alcohol within 4 hours before or after; heavy meals within 2 hours before

## 2. Cold Plunge / Cryotherapy

**Positioning:** "Cold exposure produces a norepinephrine spike of 200–300%, activates brown adipose tissue thermogenesis, drives mitochondrial biogenesis, and produces a dopamine elevation that lasts 2–4 hours post-session. Daily cold plunge is one of the most reliable, non-pharmaceutical mood and energy interventions documented. For men in the 35–60 window, it also directly counters the age-related decline in cold-adaptive capacity and brown fat activity."

**Evidence quality:** Solid. Strong mechanistic literature (Søberg et al., 2021 on brown fat and NE; van Marken Lichtenbelt on metabolic activation; Huberman lab synthesis). Longer-term mortality data is thinner than sauna. Safety profile is well established; risks are well-defined and manageable.

### Education content

**Recommended cadence (Søberg protocol):** 11 minutes total cold immersion per week, ideally split across 3–4 sessions. Do not exceed without adaptation. Quality of cold matters more than total duration at low temps.

**Temperature progression (beginner → advanced):**
- Week 1–2: 60°F (cool shower, not true plunge) — acclimate the nervous system
- Week 3–4: 55°F — first true cold plunge sensation
- Week 5–8: 50°F — full therapeutic cold stress response
- Week 9+: 39–45°F — peak protocol, maximum NE + brown fat activation

**Safety — non-negotiable:**
- Never alone for first 20+ sessions
- Never after alcohol (cardiovascular risk)
- Never with known cardiac arrhythmia without physician clearance
- Breathe before entry; controlled breathing throughout — do not hyperventilate beforehand
- Exit if extremities lose sensation

## 3. Hot Bath (Mineral)

**Positioning:** "For members who can't access a sauna, mineral-rich hot baths approximate similar physiology — vasodilation, heat shock protein induction, parasympathetic activation, and transdermal mineral absorption. Higher accessibility, lower equipment cost. Especially valuable for members with circulation issues, sauna intolerance, or travel schedules that interrupt dry-heat protocols. The evidence base is weaker than sauna but the mechanism is plausible and the risk profile is essentially zero."

**Evidence quality:** Moderate. Cardiovascular benefits of hot bath are established (Laukkanen, 2018 data includes bathing; Japanese sento/onsen epidemiological data). Transdermal magnesium absorption is debated in the literature — studies show detectable skin uptake but bioavailability relative to oral supplementation is unclear. Clinical pragmatism: the mechanism is plausible, the product is safe, the cost is low.

## 5. DIY Build Guide — TJ's Texas Outdoor Cold Plunge

TJ flagged this specific use case: building an outdoor cold plunge using a horse trough next to his pool, using a commercial ice machine, for year-round use in Texas summers. This guide documents the build for internal reference and is the basis for a future blog post or `/solutions/environment/cold-plunge` page.

### Components

**The trough:**
- Source: Tractor Supply Co. or Rural King — 100–150 gallon galvanized round-end livestock tank
- Size guidance: 100-gallon fits one person seated; 150-gallon allows legs fully extended
- Cost: $200–$300 depending on size and local pricing
- Note: galvanized steel is durable and does not leach meaningfully into cold water at these exposure durations

**Ice production options (choose one):**

| Option | Ice output | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Undercounter commercial ice maker (Manitowoc, Hoshizaki, Scotsman) | 50–100 lbs/day | $1,200–$2,500 new; $600–$1,500 refurbished | Best option for Texas summers; refurbished restaurant-supply units are reliable and well-documented |
| Chest freezer + ice block cycling | ~40 lbs/day equivalent | $300–$600 | Slower; works in mild climates; not ideal for Texas June–September |
| Bag ice purchase | Variable | ~$3–$5/bag ongoing | Viable for 2–3x/week protocol; cost adds up quickly at daily use |

For Texas summer conditions: a 150-gallon trough exposed to 95°F ambient air will climb temperature quickly. A commercial ice maker producing 80 lbs/day is the minimum viable solution for maintaining 50–55°F. Supplement with an insulated cover (reflectix wrap cut to lid shape) between sessions.

**Insulation:**
- Spray foam jacket on exterior of galvanized tank: ~$60–$80 in materials + DIY labor
- Reflectix wrap as outer layer: ~$25–$40
- Insulated lid: foam board cut to size, covered in reflectix, with cutout for access — ~$30
- Combined, this reduces temperature rise by ~30–40% in direct sun

**Drain valve:**
- Standard 3/4-inch brass garden hose bib threaded into drilled tank side — ~$12 parts; plumber or DIY drill + rubber gasket seal

**Water treatment — no chlorine:**
Chlorine is an endocrine disruptor at chronic exposure levels; avoid for a plunge used daily.

| Option | Mechanism | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ozone treatment | UV ozone generator (Intex or similar) clips to tub; kills bacteria without chemical residue | $50–$150 for unit | Best option; residue-free; runs 1–2 hr/day |
| Bromine tabs | Slower-release oxidizer than chlorine; lower volatility, less irritating | ~$15–$25/month | Acceptable; not ideal if daily immersion |
| Drain + refill weekly | No treatment chemicals; full water exchange | Water cost only | Practical in regions with low water cost; environmentally reasonable for outdoor tank |

Recommended: ozone generator + weekly partial water change. Test with a simple ORP meter ($30–$50) to confirm sanitization.

**Maintenance schedule:**
- Daily: run ozone unit 1 hr before session; check temperature
- Weekly: scrub interior with food-safe brush; partial drain (25%) + refill
- Monthly: full drain + clean with dilute hydrogen peroxide (no bleach); inspect drain fitting
- Seasonally: galvanized interior inspection; treat any surface rust with food-safe epoxy coating

**Total build cost estimate:**
- Trough: $250
- Insulation kit: $150
- Drain valve: $15
- Ozone unit: $100
- Commercial ice maker (refurbished): $800–$1,200
- **Total: $1,315–$1,715** — functional daily outdoor cold plunge next to a Texas pool

This guide becomes the basis for a future `/solutions/environment/cold-plunge-diy` content page and will drive affiliate commissions on ice machine, trough, and ozone unit purchases.
