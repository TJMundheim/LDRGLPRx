# Count Yourself Skinny: Zero-Budget Launch Plan (2026-11-05)

**Status: research and drafts only. Nothing here has been sent, posted, submitted or signed up for. TJ approves every outbound item.**

**Companion doc:** [pricing and go/no-go](cys-pricing-and-go-no-go-2026-11-05.md), written in parallel. This plan uses its price ($0.99 a month, $4.99 a year, no Apple trial, first chew-counted meal free), its gates, and its Jan 31 "spear" decision, and it does not repeat its Apple rules review.

**The short version**
- **Bet on one dare: "What's your chew number?"** It takes one bite, costs nothing, and needs no app. The AirPods demo is the on-camera proof that gets us on TV and in the tech press. It is not what most people will use.
- **The public launch is Monday, January 4, 2027.** The book goes on sale by about Nov 27 and the app by about Dec 8, so both collect ratings and reviews first. The first public videos go up Jan 1.
- **Top three channels:** (1) a January TV and local press demo, (2) short "what's your number?" videos, TJ's own plus small creators, (3) a one-hour tech press pitch for credibility.
- **Nothing pulls TJ off the blitz.** Through Nov 20 he spends 3 hours or less in total on CYS, and only on things with a waiting period.
- **The honest money:** the app makes almost nothing at this price. My base case for the 90 days after launch is about $2,750 across app, book and My4MLife. CYS is a way to reach people and fill the Contact list. It won't earn $1M on its own at this price.

## 1. The strategy and the wedge

**The strategy in one paragraph.** With no budget and 58 contacts, we can't buy attention, so we have to earn it once and catch it properly. The one thing we have that nobody else is using is a question people can't resist answering about themselves: how many times do you chew before you swallow? Nobody knows, everyone can find out in one bite, and everyone wants to dare the person across the table. We launch that question in the first week of January, the biggest diet moment of the year, with TJ running a public 12-week "Count With Me". AirPods counting chews live is the spectacle that gets producers and tech writers to say yes. The free Bite Pacer and the book are what people actually use. The money path (book on Amazon, app on the App Store, email capture, the My4MLife next door) goes live first, by mid-December, as TJ's standing rule requires.

**Why the question and not the AirPods.** This is the most important thing the research changed.

### The grocery rack answer

TJ asked whether we can do real-estate-guide-style free racks. **Not now, and not in that form.**

1. **Those racks aren't free.** Publishers pay. One paper was [paying $280 a month for rack space at Meijer, which then banned free publications](https://aan.org/?p=5881325). [Walmart stopped renting rack space in all its stores](https://www.westsidepioneer.com/Articles/051508/WalMart.html). In St. Louis, [a third-party firm charged publishers monthly](https://www.stlpr.org/government-politics-issues/2011-08-29/proposed-plan-on-distribution-of-free-publications-racks-up-opposition). Those examples are old, and I found no current rate card.
2. **Real-estate guides are paid for by their advertisers. Ours would be paid for by us.** An author copy is [about $2.30 plus shipping](../book-cys/kdp-listing.md), and [one author's shipping and tax nearly doubled his cost](https://justpublishingadvice.com/proof-and-author-copies-on-kdp/). A thousand copies is several thousand dollars.
3. **The math fails at this price.** Even at $1 a copy (offset printing, my unverified guess), with 2% scanning and 2% of those paying $4.27, each $1 copy returns about a fifth of a cent in subscriptions.
4. **Checkout placement** through wholesalers needs a sales record we don't have (from the profit model; I couldn't verify terms).

**The version worth testing later:** a free 16-page "Count to 32" mini-guide, paid for the way real-estate guides are, by a local sponsor. Start with one QR counter card at one independent grocer, which costs almost nothing, and measure scans. Revisit only after 1,000 scans from cards prove people act on them.

## 4. The dated sequence

**Yes, January is the real launch target. Monday, January 4, 2027.**
- January is [the busiest time of year for health and fitness apps](https://www.mobileaction.co/blog/new-year-resolutions-health-fitness-apps/) (source from 2019; it puts the peak at January 2), and [January 2025 set a record for in-app revenue](https://sensortower.com/blog/state-of-mobile-health-and-fitness-in-2025). So the app has to be live and rated **before** then.
- **Why Jan 4 and not the pricing doc's Jan 1:** it's the same push. New Year's Day is a Friday holiday with thin newsrooms, so the first dare videos go up Jan 1, and Day 1 of Count With Me and the TV ask land on the first Monday.
- Friday, January 8 is [Quitter's Day](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/quitters-day-2027), [originally from Strava data](https://cyclingweekly.com/news/strava-says-new-years-resolutions-going-waver-now-unless-take-action-365223), a free news hook. Treat it lightly: the often-quoted "80%" stat has no primary source, so don't use it.
- [CES runs January 6 to 9](https://www.nordicsemi.com/Events/2027/CES-2027), so tech writers will be buried. Pitch them **Dec 8 to 18**, when news is slower, or after Jan 11.
- **Why not earlier:** nothing is on sale, the blitz owns November, and Thanksgiving week is dead.
- **Why not later:** by February the moment has passed. The next natural moment is spring.

**If Apple slips:** Jan 4 still happens with the book and the web Chew Number page. The app joins when approved.

## 5. A viral loop in the product, smallest possible

**Three candidate app changes. No more.** None needs a server. No counts leave the phone unless the user shares a card.

**Candidate 1: The Chew Number test (free, about 60 seconds).**
- New `ChewNumberView`, offered at first run and from Home. One bite: tap once per chew, or if compatible AirPods are connected, use the free first meal that the pricing doc already recommends.
- Result screen: "Your number: 11. The book's number: 32." No grade, no "good" or "bad".
- Leads into `BitePacerView`. Uses existing `ChewSignalCore`.
- Analytics: add `chewNumberDone` to the allowed events, with **no number attached**, matching the existing rule.

**Candidate 2: A share card.**
- `ShareLink` on the result screen, rendering a local image: "My chew number: 11. What's yours?", the words "Count to 32", and a short link.
- **Not on the card:** weight, steps, or any health number other than the chew number. No "skinny" hashtag.
- Voluntary, and it unlocks nothing ([Apple 3.1.2(a), 3.2.2(x)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).
- **One check:** [5.1.3(i)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) bars using Motion data for marketing. The user choosing to share their own number should be fine (my reading), and the app sends nothing, but the tap-count version avoids the question altogether.
- Analytics: `shareTapped`, plus a campaign link in the card so we can count returns.
- The web page does the same thing for Android.

**Candidate 3: On-camera mode.**
- In `MealView`, one tap turns the count into a single enormous number readable across a TV studio. Available during the free first meal, so creators and producers need no code.

**What I'm not recommending:** leaderboards, streak contests, referral credits, friend lists, in-app video recording. Leaderboards in particular are unsafe here (section 8).

**Be honest about the size.** If 10% of test-takers share a card and each share brings in 0.2 installs, the loop factor is 0.02. That's a small lift and not a chain reaction. These are worth doing because each costs Claude under a day, and every press hit and TV segment gets more out of them. **A true viral loop needs a factor near 1, and nothing in our evidence says we'd reach it.**

If they threaten the Dec 1 submission, they ship as 1.1 before Dec 20. The web page version doesn't depend on Apple at all.

## 8. Blunt warnings

### The real risks

1. **Counting chews is a listed warning sign for eating disorders.** The [National Eating Disorders Association lists excessive chewing as a food ritual](https://www.nationaleatingdisorders.org/warning-signs-and-symptoms) (the page blocked my fetch; confirmed through search listings). [Romper's list names chewing a specific number of times](https://www.romper.com/p/7-early-warning-signs-of-eating-disorder-that-are-easy-to-miss-9879254). Combined with "Skinny" in the title, **this is the story a critic would write,** and it would be partly fair. The pricing doc rates it RED. This plan's chew dare raises the stakes, so:
   - 18+ rating. A plain safety step at first run and on the Chew Number screen.
   - 32 is a ceiling, not a score. The app never praises more than 32. No leaderboards, no "best chewer". A gentle "more isn't better" note.
   - Never market to minors, and never film them.
   - A short, calm statement on the press page, written in advance. If criticized, say what the safeguards are, once, and don't argue.
2. **TikTok may treat the word "skinny" as a problem.** [It blocked #SkinnyTok in 2025](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/tiktok-now-blocks-search-results-for-skinnytok-222836828.html) and [sends those searches to eating disorder resources](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/national-international/what-is-skinnytok-why-did-tiktok-ban-it-from-search/6291009/?amp=1). I couldn't verify whether the word alone reduces reach. Test one post. Lead with **"Count to 32"** everywhere on social, and let the title show on the cover in frame. It's also what you can say in a church, a clinic or an office.
3. **The title has commercial precedent and real cost.** [*Skinny Bitch* climbed the charts after Victoria Beckham was photographed carrying it](https://www.adweek.com/galleycat/?p=6233), but that was 2007, and it took a celebrity we don't have. Controversy can sell. We can't summon it and can't control it. Keep the existing rule: provocation on the cover, disarm in the content.
4. **A live demo can fail in front of everyone.** The app has [never counted a real chew](../HANDOFF.md), and talking adds false counts. TV anchors talk. Rehearse, prefer pre-tape, and say "estimate" before they do.
5. **The evidence is narrower than the pitch sounds.** The Iowa State trial was a [lab pizza lunch with 45 adults](https://www.drsharma.ca/does-increased-chewing-reduce-food-intake), it measured one meal, and a [follow-up in older adults found no change in meal size](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65dfbff663b8185d9cae90e2) (small, 18 people). The book says this honestly ("one lever in a stack"). So must TJ. Never turn it into a weight promise.
6. **Reporters will search TJ.** Use the [existing media prep](../launch/media/media-targets-2026-10-31.md) and the if-asked statement. Add the AI question, because the [KDP form](../book-cys/kdp-listing.md) discloses AI text. A clinician's name on an AI-written diet book is a bad story if someone else breaks it, and a decent one if TJ says it first. His call.
7. **"Sponsored" morning-show slots.** Local lifestyle shows [sell segments](../launch/media/media-targets-2026-10-31.md). Pay for nothing.
8. **Ododok added calories, and Apple could ship this.** Good for us on the first, bad on the second. The [counter is marketing, not the moat](cys-competitive-analysis-2026-09-19.md).
