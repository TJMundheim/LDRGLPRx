## 1. Bottom line and recommended path

**Ghostwriter is the wrong word for what TJ described.** A ghostwriter is hidden. The whole value here is the planner's visible name, reputation, and audience, so this document treats the role as a credited co-author. A hidden arrangement would also be dangerous for the planner: FINRA tells firms that representatives ["may not suggest (or encourage others to suggest)"](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/08-27) they wrote investment-related books they did not write. Whoever gets credit must actually write their pages.

**Recommendation: run two products, in this order, and keep them separate.**

1. **v1 ships on schedule** as Dr. TJ's book with Marla's foreword. Nothing waits on v2. Make the handful of small v1 changes in Section 3.
2. **Advisor Edition (structure b) comes first, starting in Q1 2027.** Same core text, with a signed "From your advisor" page the advisor writes personally. The advisor is credited as the author of that page, never as an author of the book. Small print runs, with a 16-page excerpt booklet (structure c) as the low-friction way in. This is where "educate their client base" happens at scale.
3. **One credited co-author (structure a, self-published second edition) comes next, by about Q4 2027.** Recruit a planner with a real audience who can reach Texas. The first call is **Roger Whitney**, whose site lists Fort Worth, with 9M+ podcast downloads and an existing episode on clients' fear of "losing their minds." He writes the money chapters. TJ keeps Part III. Marla's foreword stays.

**Why they must stay separate:** no planner will hand clients a book with a rival planner's name on the cover. A consumer co-author edition and a many-advisor edition are different products, so the co-author's chapters must not appear in the Advisor Edition. The deal terms in Section 6 protect that split.

**Three findings that change the original idea:**

- **The AUM retention math is weak, and it should not lead.** On my illustrative $1.5M household at about 1%, a full dementia course costs the planner about $19K in fees over ten years, and about $53K over fifteen. Across a 100-household practice, the fees the protocol might preserve come to about **$560 to $2,800 a year** under the labeled assumptions in Section 4. One new $1M client is about $10,000 a year. The real motives are client acquisition, getting the spouse and children into the room, and having a good answer to a problem advisors say they are untrained for. The [September memo](../launch/uninsured-decade/why-intermediaries-promote-2026-09-19.md) should be reordered to match.
- **The biggest legal flag is money tied to the health side.** Any payment or credit to a planner tied to clients taking the assessment, booking a consult, buying a product, or joining the Captain's Table runs into [Tex. Occ. Code § 102.001](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._occ._code_section_102.001), which is criminal, plus CFP Board referral-compensation rules. Book money can only be a flat book deal. See Section 5.
- **"Huge following" and "reachable by a no-budget author" rarely overlap.** Chatzky and Orman fit but are long shots. The Section 7 ranking rewards people who can actually be reached.

**Fallback:** if a planner's compliance department says no, use a financial journalist. [AgeProof](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/jean-chatzky/ageproof/9781478941149/) paired journalist Jean Chatzky with Dr. Michael Roizen. [Cameron Huddleston](https://cameronhuddleston.com/) cared for her mother through Alzheimer's and wrote the book on this conversation. If she holds no securities registration (verify), the FINRA and SEC layers largely fall away.

## 2. Edition structures compared

Reach figures are my planning estimates, not forecasts. The [channel plan](uninsured-decade-channel-plan-2026-09-07.md) goal of 20 intermediaries × 50 clients gives the base case.

| | (a) Co-authored edition, one named planner | (b) Advisor Edition: same core, many advisors | (c) Licensed excerpt or booklet | (d) New co-authored book instead of a new edition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Second edition: Dr. TJ and planner on the cover, planner writes the money chapters | Core book, advisor-written page, firm disclosures, "Compliments of" imprint, small runs | About 16 pages: Part I excerpt plus Week 4 checklist, firm name on the back | New title, new premise, shared IP |
| **Effort for TJ** | High: 3–6 months of collaboration, editing, compliance rounds | Medium: one template, one review pack, order handling | Low: 1–2 weeks after v1 locks | Very high: a full new manuscript |
| **Reach** | One audience, potentially tens of thousands of listeners; one advisor's clients | 20 advisors × 50–100 copies = 1,000–2,000 households, very targeted | Largest copy count, shallowest read | Largest if a publisher or big name is involved |
| **Credibility** | Highest for consumers | High with each advisor's own clients; little on the open market | Moderate | Highest, if the partner is big |
| **Risk** | Competitors won't distribute it; the planner's compliance can reshape text; a disciplinary event hits the brand | [FINRA name-on-cover problem](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/08-27) if done badly; many firms' reviews; low margin | Low; the leanest compliance review because Part III and product mentions are gone | Control of the honesty spine; long timeline; publisher's legal read of health claims |
| **Timeline** | 9–12 months after signing | First pilot in about 90 days | 30 days | 18–24 months (my estimate; typical of traditional publishing) |
| **Cost** | Editing, ISBN, cover; split with partner | Setup is mostly time; printing is covered by advisor orders | Almost nothing | Agent, proposal, or vendor fees |

### What real programs show

- **Stonewood Financial is the closest live precedent for (b).** It sells advisors a [custom edition for $1,299, including 50 copies](https://www.stonewoodfinancial.com/books/custom-book-program/), with [extra copies at $12](https://pages.stonewoodfinancial.com/custombooks) (the program page also lists $14.99). The advisor's name appears on the cover as author of the foreword, plus headshot and bio on the back. The foreword is pre-written and vendor-described as "compliance-reviewed." Use it as the price anchor. **Do not copy the pre-written foreword.** A real advisor writing a real page is both safer and more honest.
- **Power of Zero is the precedent for territory rights.** David McKnight's program packages a book with [turnkey marketing and territory exclusivity](https://www.figmarketing.com/blog/the-power-of-zero-aligning-tax-free-income-planning-with-turnkey-marketing). The source is promotional, and the program draws criticism as a life-insurance sales funnel. Borrow the territory idea, not the product tie.
- **Ed Slott's group shows advisors will pay for a branded knowledge network.** Bios describe [500+ members](https://lifelonglearning.theamericancollege.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Expert-Bios_2024.pdf) (self-reported), with [training and exam requirements](https://irahelp.com/?p=19111). That's a possible year-three idea, not a launch plan.
- **AgeProof is the closest comp for (a) or (d).** [Chatzky and Roizen, "with Ted Spiker," foreword by Dr. Oz](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/jean-chatzky/ageproof/9781478941149/), Grand Central, 2017. That "with" credit is the honest form of what people usually mean by a ghostwriter.
- **Rich Dad Poor Dad is the cautionary tale.** Sharon Lechter, a CPA, was a [credited co-author](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Lechter). She sued in 2007 and [settled in 2008](https://lunch.publishersmarketplace.com/2008/09/rich_dad_suit_settledfor/). Its "Advisor" series is a set of titles by others, not a custom program for financial advisors. Lesson: write the IP, money, and exit terms before writing chapters.
- **Ultimate Sales Machine: unverified.** I found no evidence of a custom-edition or licensing program for that title. Don't cite it as a model.
- **ForbesBooks and Advantage are author-pays and don't fit.** Advantage's CEO has put a typical author's cost at [$30–35K to create, with a similar amount for marketing](https://www.financial-planning.com/advantage-media-pitches-advisers-on%20writing-books-at-FPA-retreat). [One review](https://selfpublishing.com/advantage-forbes-books/) puts Forbes Books at $125–250K (unverified). A planner could fund this themselves, but it adds nothing to the idea. [Pay-to-co-author programs marketed to advisors](https://www.celebritybrandingagency.com/financialadvisor.html) raise the questions FINRA's notice describes.
- **Bulk resellers are a route for (b) logistics.** [BookPal and Bulk Bookstore](https://bookpal.com/storyselling-for-financial-advisors-how-top-producers-sell-9780793136643) advertise custom inserts from about 25 copies. Terms for our title are unknown.

### Second edition or second book (d)?

Choose a **second edition** if the planner writes less than about 40%, the title and frame stay, and Part III stays TJ's. That is the recommendation for anyone who is not a national name.

Choose a **new book** only if a national name wants their own title and publisher. In that case, v1's Part I becomes source material, TJ owns the health half, and v1 stays in print. Don't reshape v1 around this chance.

### KDP realities

- **Own the ISBNs.** A free KDP ISBN ["cannot be used to publish outside of KDP"](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834170), and the imprint displays as "Independently published." With your own ISBN, "My4MLife Press" appears, but title, author, and publisher must match the registration exactly. A Bowker 10-pack is [reported at $295](https://selfpublishing.com/isbn-number/) (third-party source; confirm at myidentifiers.com). Ten covers paperback, hardcover, and later editions.
- **Locked fields.** [Title, primary author, and ISBN lock after publishing](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200736410), with a 72-hour fix window for print title, subtitle, and primary author. So keep Dr. TJ as primary author on v1.
- **Adding a co-author.** [Add them under Contributors with type "Author"](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G2BWJN2BY98T5PV2). KDP lets contributors change after publishing. But a new cover or a [page-count change over 10% is a new edition](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GW7J4WEKBVU25YEC), so v2 is a new listing with Edition number 2 and a new ISBN.
- **Don't change title and author together.** KDP says ["significant changes to both book title and author name aren't allowed"](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GW7J4WEKBVU25YEC). A changed title also needs "Previously published as [book title] by [author name]" in the description. Keep the title.
- **Keep the pieces matched.** Cover, title page, copyright page, and KDP metadata must agree on the co-author's name. Add a new copyright line, "Second Edition, 2027," and About the Co-Author.
- **Advisor Editions don't belong on Amazon.** Print off-KDP as "Not for resale." I found no explicit KDP ban on near-duplicates, but the [content guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390) reserve removal for what "creates a poor customer experience," and 20 near-identical listings invite that. Get two short-run quotes. The [September memo's ≈ $4 a copy](../launch/uninsured-decade/why-intermediaries-promote-2026-09-19.md) is unconfirmed for custom runs.

**What Marla keeps:** the foreword, "A Word About Marla," and a legal read of Spend-down, the POA and directive lines, and Week 4. She does not co-author, and she should not be listed as reviewing anything financial.

**The "From your advisor" page:**

- **Placement:** right after "A Word About Marla."
- **Length:** 150 words, written and signed by the advisor.
- **Fixed parts:** firm name, required disclosures, and a statement of how they are paid.
- **No** performance claims, no product names, no health claims.
- **Two versions:**
  - *Compliments of*: an educational gift that offers no services, which is more likely to stay outside the SEC's definition of advertisement.
  - *Firm page*: invites a meeting, so treat it as an advertisement and run it through compliance. Attorney's call on each.

### What to change in v1 now

All of these are small, none delays publication, and none is made yet. They are for TJ's decision.

1. **Buy the ISBN block** and publish under My4MLife Press. Never use the free KDP ISBN.
2. **Get a one-page permission from Marla** covering later editions and the Advisor Edition. Without it, her words may not carry over.
3. **Add a "Questions to bring to your advisor" page** right after the foreword section. It is useful now, and it becomes the "From your advisor" page later without shifting the pagination.
4. **Move the Captain's Table out of Ch 13** into one back-matter page, and make the /coaching line in "The Assessment" removable. An advisor's compliance reviewer will stop at a $50K program offer in the middle of a chapter.
5. **Fix the handed-you language for Amazon buyers.** Ch 1 says "the person who put this book in your hands is the one who protects that folder," which is untrue for anyone who bought it themselves. Add "your advisor, or the one you are about to find" in the key places.
6. **Tag voices in the source.** Add invisible markers in `_MASTER.md` (`<!-- voice:money -->`, `<!-- voice:health -->`, `<!-- slot:advisor -->`). That makes the later split mechanical.
7. **Catch these before v1 prints.** A planner would spot them, and so will a careful reader:
   - **"Three things that tip the ledger" (Ch 7) only names "One."** Two and Three appear to have been lost in the cut from the long draft.
   - **Ch 1 calls 42% "a coin flip that leans against you."** At 42% (48% for women) it leans the other way. The couple's ~66% is what leans against you.
   - **"I told you on page five" (Signing) and "Read the first six pages" (Who This Book Is For)** are hard page references, and the foreword will move them. Check them against the final layout.

## 4. The planner's motivation, honestly

### The math

**Assumptions, all mine and illustrative:**
- $1.5M portfolio at the start of the course.
- 1% fee on the start-of-year balance. A [2017 survey via Kitces](https://www.kitces.com/blog/independent-financial-advisor-fees-comparison-typical-aum-wealth-management-fee/) put the median at 1.00% up to $1M and 0.85% above it, and later write-ups report much the same. So a flat 1% flatters these numbers.
- 5% net growth before fees.
- $60,000 a year living draw, rising 3%.
- Care spending is added on top.

| Scenario for one affected household | Fees lost, 10 yrs | Fees lost, 15 yrs | Balance vs. baseline, end of yr 10 | Annual fee in yr 11 (baseline $13,527) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Book's ledger, Ch 3 midpoint** (~$527,500 over 10 years) | ~$18,700 | ~$52,700 | down ~$628,000 | $7,250 |
| **$405,262 spread evenly** over 10 years | ~$21,300 | ~$48,900 | down ~$510,000 | $8,430 |
| **$100,000 a year** for years 6–10 | ~$10,900 | ~$41,700 | down ~$568,000 | $7,846 |
| **$120,000 a year for 10 years** (tail case) | ~$63,100 | ~$127,200 | account at $0 | $0 |

**Caution on $405,262.** The Alzheimer's Association figure includes [unpaid caregiving and is 70% family-borne](../book-uninsured-decade/draft/_MASTER.md), so it overstates what leaves the portfolio.

**What it means for a practice.** Take 100 households at $1.5M: $150M AUM and about $1.5M in annual revenue. Assume 10–20% enter a paid-care course in the decade, 10–25% of clients actually run the protocol, and the book's 30% risk reduction holds.

- **Expected fees preserved: about $5,600 to $28,000 over ten years, or $560 to $2,800 a year.**
- Each of those three inputs is an assumption. The 30% is an aggregate the book itself says [no single trial has produced](../book-uninsured-decade/draft/_MASTER.md).

**Compare what else moves the needle:**

- **One new $1M client** is about $10,000 a year. Kitces puts typical 2026 acquisition cost at [$2,551 per client and $0.70 per dollar of new revenue](https://www.kitces.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Kitces-Research-Study-Advisor-Marketing-2026.pdf).
- **Advisor time is the real barrier.** The same report says time is about 60% of marketing cost and that book writing fell as advisors moved away from time-heavy tactics. About 6% of advisors use a book, with average satisfaction of 5.3; cost data isn't shown because of too few responses. A nearly finished book removes that barrier. That is the honest pitch.
- **The whole relationship is the larger exposure.** Cerulli says [more than 70% of heirs are likely to fire or change advisors](https://www.cerulli.com/press-releases/aging-boomers-bring-intergenerational-planning-to-the-forefront), and [a similar share of women are reported to change after a spouse dies](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2aucrubf4azh6wteoto8w/ria-intel/how-advisors-should-prepare-for-the-clients-inheriting-72-6-trillion) (secondary source; the "within one year" version I could not trace to Cerulli). Losing the whole $1.5M account costs about $13,500 a year. Week 4 puts both spouses and the printed plan in the advisor's office, which helps **even if the protocol does nothing medically**.
- **Advisors say they need this.** In [MIT AgeLab's Q2 2024 brief](https://agelab.mit.edu/static/uploads/june-brain-health-survey.pdf) (83 financial professionals, a small sample), 75% had a client receive a dementia diagnosis, 41% had a client with significant losses tied to decline, 25% had specific training, and 93% wanted more.
- **Late handoffs cost clients.** Vanguard investors in a [Center for Retirement Research brief](https://crr.bc.edu/transferring-control-of-finances-timing-poses-a-risk/) estimated that a too-late transfer of control would cost about 18% of their wealth (their own estimate, not a measured loss). [Nicholas et al., JAMA Internal Medicine](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33252621/), found missed payments up to six years before diagnosis. This is the book's natural second topic for a planner.

**So the corrected order of motives:**

1. Acquisition.
2. Spouse and next-generation relationship.
3. Competence on a problem they already face.
4. Duty.
5. Fee retention, a distant fifth.

### The honest limit

**Never tell a planner, or anyone, that the protocol keeps assets invested.** No evidence supports that.

- The book's spine says the odds shift, it doesn't break even, and it's [ruin avoidance, not ROI](../book-uninsured-decade/README.md). A fee-retention claim is an ROI claim under another name.
- Avoiding dementia doesn't keep money with one advisor. People spend it, live longer, and give it away.
- The protocol costs money too, $3–5K a year new for most households, per the book.
- **There's also a conflict.** A fee on assets pays an advisor more when money stays invested. Paying for care, buying coverage, or gifting to family may be right for the client and bad for the fee. The planner's first duty runs to the client's plan.

I found no source for the [channel plan's](uninsured-decade-channel-plan-2026-09-07.md) line that cognitive decline is "the #1 cause of an AUM relationship blowing up." Don't repeat it.

### Private motive, public message

"Your clients keep their money with you longer" stays out of the book, and it is not the reader-facing pitch.

- **Between TJ and the planner:** "Decline is a real threat to your clients and your relationships. Here's a book that lets you raise it. I can't promise it changes your AUM."
- **To the reader:** "This is the biggest unfunded line in your plan. Your advisor helps fund and structure it. The work in Part III may lower the odds."

### Telling readers about the planner's interest

Follow Marla's model. She opens with her two conflicts and says, "Weigh everything I say here with both of those facts in mind." The planner does the same on the first page of their section:

> **DRAFT.** Before you read my chapters, you should know how I get paid. I earn a fee based on the assets my firm manages, which may include yours. If your money stays invested, I earn more; if you spend it on care, I earn less. Weigh my advice with that in mind. I receive [a royalty on store sales / no royalty] from this book. Neither I nor my firm is paid anything if you take the assessment, use My4MLife, or see any clinician this book refers you to. I wrote the chapters on money. I did not write or review the health chapters, and I am not a clinician. I checked Dr. TJ's arithmetic in Chapter 7; I did not check his medicine.

## 5. Compliance realities to check with counsel

**These are issues for an attorney, not conclusions.** I read the sources below, but I'm not a lawyer and this is not a legal opinion. Each planner's firm decides for itself.

| Topic | Source | Practical point |
|---|---|---|
| **Referral money, health side (biggest flag)** | [Tex. Occ. Code § 102.001](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._occ._code_section_102.001) reaches paying "directly or indirectly, overtly or covertly" for steering patients to a state-licensed provider; a Class A misdemeanor, a felony on repeat. See also [§ 102.004](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._occ._code_section_102.004) (advertising carve-out) and [ch. 102](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/OC/pdf/OC.102.pdf) (federal safe-harbor construction, disclosure). [Federal AKS](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/1320a-7b) for federal-program business. [18 U.S.C. § 220](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/220) (EKRA) for labs; [covers private payers](https://www.maynardnexsen.com/publication-New-All-Payor-Anti-Kickback-Law-in-Opioid-Bill-Impacts-Clinical-Laboratories); secondary sources conflict on cash-pay | **No fee, credit, discount, or gift to a planner tied to the assessment, consult, labs, products, or Captain's Table.** Matches the [channel map rule](captains-table-channel-map-2026-10-31.md). Consults and labs flow to licensed practices, so the risk is real even though My4MLife itself isn't a licensed provider. Don't reuse the Push Patch partner mechanic (`/partners`) here |
| **The alternative: book economics** | [§ 102.001](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._occ._code_section_102.001); counsel to confirm | Flat per-copy royalty on retail sales only. Zero royalty on copies the planner's own firm buys or gives. Nothing varies with health-side activity. Cleanest version: no royalty at all, and the planner buys copies at cost |
| **Name on the cover (FINRA reps)** | [Reg. Notice 08-27](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/08-27) (2008; cites older NASD rules, confirm how it maps today) lists books "with the representative's name printed on the cover" and says third-party works must disclose who prepared them. ### Due diligence runs both ways

**Check them first.** Before approaching anyone, look them up on [FINRA BrokerCheck](https://brokercheck.finra.org/) and the [SEC adviser search](https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/), plus their state regulator if needed. The standing rule of no proactive mention covers public and marketing surfaces, but a co-author's firm doing diligence, with reps and warranties in a contract, is a different situation. **TJ should decide with counsel, before signing anything, how to handle this.** The private statement at `docs/legal/private/if-asked-statement.md` still has open placeholders to complete. | **Honesty clause** | **The no-break-even math and the "lowers odds, no guarantee" language can't be softened** without TJ's sign-off |
| **Term** | **5 years, renewable.** Either side can take their name off at the next printing with 90 days' notice |
| **Disciplinary exit** | **Mutual.** Rep at signing that disclosures are clean or fully listed. Notice within 5 business days of any new regulatory event. The other side can remove the name, and the party at fault pays for the reprint. Re-check BrokerCheck and the SEC adviser search each year |

| **Editing cost** | **TJ pays for developmental and copy editing of the core. Planner pays their own firm's compliance review and any help writing their chapters.** If they use a writer, credit them with "with." |
| **Marketing duties** | **Planner commits to specifics:** two podcast episodes, one newsletter, one event. Audience is the whole reason for the deal, so write it down |
| **Rich Dad clause** | Dispute resolution, audit rights on royalties, and what happens on death, incapacity, or firm sale. Yes, also incapacity |
