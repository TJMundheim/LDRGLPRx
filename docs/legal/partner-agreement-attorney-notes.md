# Partner Agreement PA-2026-10: Notes for Attorney Review

**Internal only. Do not show to partners.**
Document: docs/legal/My_4M_Life_Partner_Agreement.md · Prepared 2026-10-31 for TJ's counsel.

Program in one line: IV bars, med spas and facial spas share a link or code for the Push Patch, a cash-pay prescription product. A network physician approves or declines every order. The partner earns a flat $100 (client gets $100 off) or $150 (client gets $50 off) per approved, paid order, and also on later refills by the same client.

Please confirm or advise on:

- **Texas patient solicitation and fee-splitting.** The commission is paid per order, and only once a physician approves a prescription. Analyze this under the Texas Patient Solicitation Act (Tex. Occ. Code ch. 102) and the Medical Practice Act's fee-splitting and referral-fee provisions (incl. § 165.155). Does tying payment to physician approval make it worse? Would a flat fee or a fee per lead instead be safer? Also consider partners with licensed staff (an RN, NP or med-spa medical director), whose own boards bar referral fees.
- **Federal and other-state exposure.** The product is cash-pay only, with no federal health care program billing, so we assume the federal Anti-Kickback Statute does not reach it. Please confirm. Also confirm whether shipping to clients outside Texas brings in other states' solicitation, fee-splitting or "all-payer" anti-kickback laws.
- **Refill attribution.** The partner keeps earning on refills for as long as the agreement lasts. Recurring pay on prescription refills may weigh more heavily in the solicitation analysis than a one-time fee. Should refill commissions be capped, time-limited or dropped?
- **Physician independence and corporate practice of medicine.** Confirm that partner commissions sit entirely outside physician pay and decisions. The reviewing physician should not know which partner referred an order. Confirm too that My 4M Life's structure with its physician network is sound under Texas corporate-practice rules.
- **Clawback mechanics.** Refunds and chargebacks are clawed back for 30 days after payment, and recovery is only by offset against future payouts. Chargebacks can land 60 to 120 days later. Should the window be longer? Should there be a right to direct repayment when no future payouts exist?
- **1099-NEC and Stripe Connect.** We understand the reporting threshold is $2,000 for payments made in 2026 and later (it was $600); please verify. Confirm that payouts made through Stripe Connect (Express) are reported on a 1099-NEC, not a 1099-K, and that Stripe can file it for us. Confirm our backup-withholding duty if a partner never gives a valid TIN. Confirm that letting Stripe hold the W-9 meets our record-keeping duties.
- **Trademarks and advertising.** Confirm who owns the "Push Patch" mark and whether it is registered. Confirm that we may license its use to partners. Is the ban on paid ads using our trademarks (brand bidding) enforceable? Do any federal or Texas rules limit advertising a discount on a prescription product?
- **FTC endorsement disclosure.** We added a duty for partners to tell clients they earn a commission (16 CFR Part 255). Should the agreement say how and where that disclosure must appear (staff script, counter card, social posts)?
- **Entity name, signature and e-sign formation.** The agreement uses "My 4M Life," as in the other legal docs. Please confirm the exact legal entity (LLC? a d/b/a of Essential Management Group?) and that "Dr. TJ, Founder" is an acceptable signature. Signing works by typed name plus an "I agree to sign electronically" checkbox, with an audit record (doc SHA-256, IP, user agent, timestamp). The counter-signature is applied "on acceptance." Confirm this forms a contract under E-SIGN and Texas UETA. Partners are businesses, so we assume E-SIGN's consumer-consent disclosures do not apply; please confirm.
- **Risk allocation gaps (kept out for simplicity).** The agreement has no indemnity from the partner, for example for TCPA or CAN-SPAM claims or unapproved health claims. It has no cure period before termination for breach, and our liability is capped at commissions owed while the partner's is not. We may change commissions on 30 days' notice; is that clause at risk of being "illusory" under Texas law? Advise whether a short partner indemnity is worth the extra length.
